Pokémon Champions has finally been released on mobile devices, opening the game up to a ton of new players. The game also has a new rule set that includes the addition of items, Pokémon, and new mega evolutions. However, despite the addition of the new content, the game still kind of feels the same. Most, if not all of the best performing Pokémon in the previous rule set, regulation MA, are continuing to dominate the meta game in regulation [music] MB. This doesn't mean that the new mega evolutions are bad at all. In fact, some of these mega evolutions have the potential to be meta defining when paired with the right partners. In competitive Pokémon, these groups are called cores and are the foundation of any team. If you're looking to try something new this regulation, here are three cores built around three of the new mega evolutions that have not only seen success in the game so far, but are also exciting to use and easy for beginner players to learn. Let's start by defining what a core is in VGC. A core is a group of two to four Pokémon that synergize extremely well with each other. This synergy is a defining factor on how the entire team functions during battles and can even determine the composition of the rest of the team. There are many different types of cores in competitive Pokémon. For example, a classic core in VGC is the fire water grass core, which includes at least one Pokémon of each of these three types. These three types work extremely well together because each type can cover the weaknesses of the other two. A recent example is the core of Incineroar, Rillaboom, and Urshifu Rapid Strike from the Scarlet and Violet era. This core was a staple on most balanced teams since these three Pokémon provided great offensive pressure, utility, and support, not just for each other, but for the rest of the team. A core doesn't have to be type based. It can be based on a battle mechanic. Throughout the history of competitive Pokémon, there have been cores based around weather, terrain, and speed control. An example of this in Pokémon Champions is the core of Mega Charizard Y and a Tailwind setter. Charizard Y deals absurd damage and can summon harsh sunlight with its ability drought, but its base 100 speed allows it to be outsped by powerful threats. A Tailwind setter like Aerodactyl or Whimsicott pairs well with Charizard Y, not only due to the speed boost from Tailwind, but also the offensive coverage or support moves they can provide to complement Charizard. Fast and powerful Pokémon like Garchomp and Basculegion complement this core very well and can form the foundation of a powerful hyper offense team. The last type of core I want to mention is a core centered around a specific Pokémon, which is the type of core I'll be focusing on in this video. The Pokémon at the center of this core is the star of the team, and in order to unlock its true potential, it needs the right partners. In Pokémon Champions, the center of this type of core is almost always a Mega Evolution. The Mega is usually the strongest Pokémon on the team, so naturally it wants to be under the perfect conditions to perform at its best. However, that doesn't mean the entire [music] core falls apart if the Mega goes down. These cores are often built with secondary attackers in mind that not only complement the Mega very well, but also benefit from the function of the supportive core members. Now that we've defined what a core is, let's get into our first example. This first core focuses on Mega Pyroar. I'll be real, I originally thought Mega Pyroar was terrible. I pretty much called it a worse Mega Charizard Y. However, after seeing it in action, I'll admit that it does have some sauce to it. It's actually one of the fastest Mega Evolutions in the game, and its special attack stat, while not massive, is still pretty good. What makes Mega Pyroar stand out is its signature ability, Fire Main. Fire Main causes Mega Pyroar's Fire type attacks to deal 50% more damage. While at first glance like a weaker version of Adaptability, Fire Main is actually added to the stat boost, effectively giving Mega Pyroar's Fire type moves a 2.25 times damage boost over Adaptability's two times boost. While Pyroar's Fire type damage is impressive, it can be boosted even further by the sun. A sun setter with the ability Drought serves as the second element of this core. However, this is where the core kind of splits in two. Depending on the sun setter you choose, the core and the rest of the team will be completely different. The first option is Ninetales. Ninetales isn't really a special Pokémon on its own due to its average stats. However, access to the ability Drought is huge. It's also pretty fast and can boost its damage by setting up with Nasty Plot. Taking the Ninetales route of this core means you'll want to use faster Pokémon on this team. Whimsicott is an excellent third member of this version of the core due to access to the move Tailwind, as well as its ability Prankster, which gives status moves like Tailwind priority. Mega Pyroar is already fast, but having its speed doubled under Tailwind helps it outspeed threats that would normally outspeed it. Speedy attackers like Garchomp, Venusaur, and Mega Floette complement this core well. As for the other route of this core, you can instead use Torkoal as your sun setter. Torkoal is much slower and more supportive compared to Ninetales. Unlike Ninetales, Torkoal has access to Helping Hand, which can boost Mega Pyroar's overall damage by 50%. Helping Hand boosted, sun boosted, fire main boosted Heat Wave or Overheat does such disgusting damage that resistances basically don't exist. Not only that, but Torkoal can also hit harder than Ninetales with Eruption. Due to Torkoal's much lower speed, this version of the core tends to work better under Forretress tends to be the last member of this core due to its great bulk, ability to block priority with Armor Tail, and its access to Trick Room. It can also learn Helping Hand, allowing it to boost Pyroar or Torkoal's damage. While you normally wouldn't want to have Mega Pyroar be under Trick Room due to its high speed, there are some scenarios where flipping the speed order might be a good idea. If you're dealing with faster Mega Evolutions or your opponent's team heavily relies on Tailwind, Trick Room can actually work in Mega Pyroar's favor. Slower attackers and supporters like Sylveon and Incineroar are great compliments to this version of the core. However you decide to build this core, the important thing is providing Mega Pyroar with sun support for a damage boost as well as the right speed control. Our next core is around Mega Raichu Y, aka Mega Pikachu. Mega Raichu Y is almost as much of a glass cannon as Pheromosa. Ridiculously high special attack stat, very high speed, but it can't take a hit to save its life. This thing doesn't take Fake Out chip, it takes Fake Out chunk. However, that doesn't really matter when you realize what it's capable of. Raichu Y has the ability No Guard, which causes all of its attacks to land. It can also learn Zap Cannon, an extremely powerful Electric type attack that also paralyzes the target. Normally, Zap Cannon has only 50% accuracy, but with No Guard, it goes from a 50/50 shot to a guaranteed paralysis, if not just a straight-up one-hit KO. It also learns Focus Blast, another move notorious for its low accuracy. But thanks to No Guard, Focus Blast goes from a Hail Mary to a very reliable coverage move. It even gets access to Fake Out itself in case it wants to protect itself from the dreaded Fake Out chunk. The only Fake Out user that outspeeds Mega Raichu Y is Mega Lopunny, so you'd have to watch out for that. Raichu Y is already extremely fast, but running it with Tailwind will almost guarantee that it won't be outsped. For that reason, a Tailwind setter like Whimsicott is the second member of this core. If Raichu-Y gets outsped, it's basically going to get one-hit KO'd, especially by physical attackers. Since there aren't many Pokémon in the game that can outspeed it naturally, your opponent will usually need Tailwind. However, if you match that Tailwind, Raichu-Y regains its speed advantage. Whimsicott is by far the best Tailwind setter for Raichu-Y, not only because of its large support move pool, but also because it can deal with Garchomp, which is one of Raichu-Y's biggest threats. Focus Blast isn't enough to get rid of Garchomp, so a Moonblast from Whimsicott is certainly appreciated. Whimsicott can even be an answer to opposing Whimsicott. Not only can you match the Tailwind, but you can also try and prevent the opposing Whimsicott from using Encore on Raichu-Y. The last thing you want happening is Raichu-Y getting locked in a Fake Out or Zap Cannon at the worst possible [music] moment. Your Whimsicott can use Taunt to prevent the opponents from using status moves, or you can use Encore yourself. The major thing to watch out for is the speed tie. Whimsicott pretty much always runs max speed, so whoever's Whimsicott goes first comes down to a coin flip. That 50/50 can be the difference between a victory and a loss. For the last member of this core, you'll want a fast physical attacker to complement Raichu's special attacks. The best options are Basculegion and Garchomp. They both provide great coverage and can deal with opponents that heavily threaten Raichu-Y. Basculegion's Wave Crash can massacre opposing ground types, while Last Respects deals with Sinistcha. Garchomp can deal with basically everything else, including other Garchomps. Look, this thing's the most used Pokémon in the game right now, so you need to be able to deal with it, especially since Raichu-Y has a terrible time against it. Seriously though, Garchomp holding a Life Orb can deal with the majority of the metagame with its high speed, offensive damage, and wide move pool. Honestly, you can run both of these guys for the core. They're very flexible, they appreciate Whimsicott's Tailwind support, and they can honestly work on any type of team. The final core I want to discuss centers around Mega Staraptor. I'm going to let you know now, I got a lot to say about this core, cuz it's the one I've been using the most in this regulation. This bird has been a dominant force these past few weeks. It's even the most used new Mega Evolution in the game right now. Staraptor on its own is is very solid Pokémon, easily the best early route bird Pokémon in the entire series. It has access to intimidate, which is one of the best abilities in competitive Pokémon, and has an amazing move pool with moves like Brave Bird, Close Combat, and U-Turn. In terms of stats, it's very strong and has decent speed, but it's pretty frail. Normal flying also isn't the best type. However, mega evolution turned it into the lethal weapon that it was destined to be. Mega Staraptor is now a fighting flying type, meaning it finally gets a stab boost for close combat. It also gains a respectable boost to its attack and speed, but more importantly, it gains respectable bulk. Why does bulk matter on a Pokémon that screams hyper offense? Because of its ability, contrary. Contrary causes all stat changes the Pokémon experiences to be reversed. This means that stat boosts become stat drops, but more importantly, stat drops become stat boosts. If we take a look at close combat, its effect is that after attacking the target, the user experiences a one stage drop to both its defense and special defense. However, because of contrary, those defense drops become defense boosts. This means you can spam close combat and keep boosting your defenses. And with that respectable bulk and access to roost for recovery, Mega Staraptor can actually become unkillable. Attack drops from intimidate become attack boosts, and speed control moves like Rock Tomb, Electro Web, and Icy Wind instead make Mega Staraptor faster. So, what Pokémon work best with Mega Staraptor? Let's start with Whimsicott. It's almost like Whimsicott's the best support Pokémon in the game. Tailwind is as helpful as always, but when it comes to Mega Staraptor, this is where the other aspects of Whimsicott's support move pool shine. Whimsicott can learn stat lowering moves like Charm and Fake Tears. If these moves are used on Mega Staraptor, contrary instead gives these moves to raise its stats. Not only that, but these moves also have boosted priority due to Prankster. Whimsicott can give Mega Staraptor a free plus two boost to its attack by using Charm, then Mega Staraptor can proceed to nuke its target with close combat, giving it a boost to both of its defenses. Just from that one turn, Mega Staraptor gets four stat boosts. Quick note, while Cotton Spore seems like a tempting option for the speed boost, you can't use it on your ally. Plus, you have Tailwind anyway, which is a better option. The last Pokémon of this core is Grimmsnarl. Just like Whimsicott, it has an excellent support move pool and the ability Prankster. However, Grimmsnarl has two major differences compared to Whimsicott. The first is the move pool. Grimmsnarl has access to Reflect and Light Screen to increase the survivability of the entire team. It's able to learn Parting Shot, which lets it pivot out, and it even has access to Fake Out. It also has a signature move in Spirit Break, which deals respectable damage and lowers the target's special attack. Second, Grimmsnarl is a Dark type, making it immune to status moves from opponents with Prankster. Being immune to Prankster Encore and Taunt is massive and helps Grimmsnarl do its job even better. Grimmsnarl can set up screens to help protect Mega Staraptor, then use Parting Shot on it not only to get off the field at the beginning of the turn, but also gives Staraptor an attack boost before it gets the move. It might not be as big of a boost as Charm, but in most cases, a plus one boost is more than enough. As for other stat-changing moves, Grimmsnarl can learn Scary Face to boost Mega Staraptor's speed, Fake Tears to raise its special defense, and even Swagger to lower the attack stat of opposing Mega Staraptor. Many players will argue that it's only necessary to run one of these Pokémon with Mega Staraptor. However, I think there are valid reasons to run both. Running only one of the support Pokémon puts all the pressure on it. That one Pokémon is responsible for boosting Mega Staraptor, and as a result, you have to build it specifically for that role. Having both Whimsicott and Grimmsnarl on your team means that you have a lot more flexibility when it comes to their move sets. For example, if you have Parting Shot on Grimmsnarl, you don't have to run Charm on Whimsicott, meaning you can still run Encore. If you have Tailwind on Whimsicott, you don't need Scary Face on Grimmsnarl, meaning you don't have to drop one of the screens or Spirit Break. Their roles are so different that depending on your opponent's team, you won't need to bring both of them into the match, allowing you to bring an extra attacker. As powerful as your Mega Evolution is, you need to have a plan B if it goes down. If Whimsicott and Grimmsnarl are your last two Pokémon, your chances of winning are often pretty slim.
