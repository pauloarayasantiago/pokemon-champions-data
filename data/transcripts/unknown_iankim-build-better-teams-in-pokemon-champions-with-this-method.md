# Build BETTER Teams in Pokemon Champions With This Method

**Channel:** IanKim | **Date:** unknown | **Views:** 2,458 | **Duration:** 6:43

If you've ever wanted to build your own team instead of copying a pro player's team, this video will showcase a team building framework I like to use when team building. And the last step is the most critical step. After looking at many teams, most teams can be broken down into a simple Venn diagram. So what exactly is a Venn diagram? It is a visual tool illustrating different groups of data and the overlapping part in the middle having something in common. An example of a Venn diagram will be the differences between a dolphin and a fish. On the left side we see dolphins' unique traits, and on the right side we see the fish's unique traits. And then in the middle we see traits that both dolphins and fish both possess. Now why am I exactly telling you this? Most teams can be thought of exactly like a Venn diagram, each circle being a core or a Pokemon. For example, we'll take a look at some of the most common cores right now. The most popular team has two cores, trials and a guard jump that launch a bunch of spread move damage and have good defensive synergy with each other. An Etrichrom core with Furiguraffe, King Gambit and Sylveon, and an Aerodactyl in the middle who can come in both cores. But Pokemon isn't one dimensional, the overlap in the middle isn't the only bridge between these cores. For example, you can run Sylveon, Furiguraffe and King Gambit alongside guard jump and Charizard. Unlike most Venn diagrams, which are black and white, these Venn diagrams for Pokemon are flexible. Here's another example, we have a fast Sun core with Pyvore and Ninetales, the wet core on the right side as a flexible core, as a flexible core herself. Then we have Whimsicott, Furiguraffe and Bascalision that can come into either cores. We'll take a look at another core, the Parrish Trap core. On one side we have the Parrish Trap enabler Gengar, and on the other side we have Swampert and Arcalodon core who want rain. Then Incineroar, Politard and Vibilon can come into either cores. And the last example we'll take a look at is the Venusaur Sun core. On one side we have the Charizard, Venusaur and guard jump as a core, and Sylveon and Furiguraffe as a core on the other side, and Incineroar can come into both cores. So what are the main takeaways with this Venn diagram framework? Firstly, most teams do have two cores, and these cores are built around Mega Evolutions for the most part. Cores will have synergies within themselves, whether that be offensive or defensive, for example Pyvore and Ninetales deal obscene amounts of fire type damage, whereas Charizard enables Venusaur's chlorophyll, and because Charizard is a flying type, it's immune to Garchomp's earthquake, and Garchomp and Charizard provide great defensive synergies too. The Pokemon in the middle of the Venn diagram are called Bridge Pokemon, and I'm not talking about Arcalodon, but instead Pokemon that can fit in both cores. For example Incineroar can be used greatly under Trick Room with its 60 base speed, but it can be brought outside of Trick Room with the amount of utility this Pokemon has. And lastly, unlike most Venn diagrams, cores aren't exclusive because competitive Pokemon is a multi-layered game, Pokemon isn't a one-dimension game, and you might not always bring Garchomp, Venusaur, and Charizard into every matchup. For example, if your opponent has a lot of priority and is running a full ice team, not running Venusaur and Garchomp is very justified, and bringing Ferograth there makes a lot of sense. Now we've got the framework down, let's talk about how to use it and its applications. The very first step to this process is to choose a Pokemon you'd like to build around. In the current format, it's very easy to choose because you can choose a Mega Evolution as your starting one piece. After you've chosen your Mega Evolution, the second step is to identify your Pokemon's strength, weaknesses, and synergies. Then if your Pokemon has any synergies, ask yourself, is these synergies good, yes or no, and why or why not? Synergies don't just have to be with abilities like Swift Swim, but it can also be defensive or offensive synergy. For example, Fire and Fairy are synonymous offensive synergies, even back in the Scarlet and Violet era with the Fluttermane and Chiyu. Whereas right now in Pokemon Champions, Incineroar and Sinistra provide a great defensive synergy with each other because Incineroar resists 4 out of 5 of Sinistra's weaknesses, Sinistra resists or is immune to 3 out of 4 of Incineroar's weaknesses. At this point, you should have one Pokemon on your team, and this should constitute as one side of your Venn Diagram. The third question to ask yourself is what meta Pokemon counters my Pokemon? Can this Pokemon handle it, and how will this Pokemon be able to handle the meta? For reference, here are the top 10 Pokemon in the game, and your first Pokemon should be able to handle a few of the top meta options. The fourth step is where your identity starts to form. You have your main mega Pokemon, you've identified the strengths and weaknesses, and now we have to bring one or two Pokemon to complete the core. This is undoubtedly the hardest process, because you'll have identified multiple synergies with your mega Pokemon, and you have many different options, so it all really comes down to trial and error. Once again, once you have your 2-3 Pokemon core set, you'll want to identify the weaknesses of this core. What is my core beat to, and what are its hard matchups? For example, with the Charizard Y Floette mega team, Floette Eternal helps Charizard against his worst nightmare matchup, a sand team with Tyranitar. And this is where the next step comes in handy. Ask yourself, is there another mega evolution I can bring onto the team that can A, help with a match that my primary core struggles with, or B, can somewhat synergize with the core I already have at hand? And this doesn't have to be a mega evolution, this can be a regular Pokemon, but generally most mega evolutions will have a weakness, and you'll want to bring on another mega evolution who can kind of hide that weakness. So now, we are on the other side of the Venn Diagram, and we're looking at Pokemon from one side and seeing if they can move into the middle. So for example on the left side, seeing if Incineroar can move into the middle. You should have around 4-5 Pokemon, and now you'll either want one or two things as your last Pokemon or Pokemon's. First, how can I patch up these remaining bad matchups with the final two Pokemon? Or if your team is already strong as is, how can I further strengthen my bridge between these two archetypes by adding Pokemon that can play both archetypes? And five, but last but not least, the most important advice is playtesting. Playtesting is the most important part of the process because your team can work on paper, but it may not work in practice because Pokemon is a very dynamic game. Test your team, go back to the drawing board, figure out what works, what your most difficult matchups are, and continue making adjustments.
