# Every Mega Evolution in Pokémon Champions Ranked (All 81)

**Channel:** VGC Vault | **Date:** unknown | **Views:** 26 | **Duration:** 13:22

---

Most mega evolutions in Pokémon champions are a waste of your one mega slot, and the data is brutal about it. There are 81 legal megas. We went through every published team list from all six regionals this format. More than half of them didn't show up on a single one. So, who actually made the cut? Four megas, and they all have a different reason for being here. But first, the one that fell the furthest. In September, it was the most feared mega in the game. A player who has won two regionals this format now calls it a solid choice. &gt;&gt; Mega Salamence won the very first regional of this format. Joseph Ugarte took it to the title in Baltimore, next to Mega Tyranitar. It has history, too. The hyper voice and double-edge set that won a big online event this September is, as Cybertron put it, basically the set that won the 2019 World Championship. &gt;&gt; Mega Gallade turns hyper voice into a flying type spread move on a Pokémon with 120 base speed and intimidate before it mega evolves. So, what went wrong? Everyone [snorts] saw it coming. As players on r/vgc put it, Salamence was public enemy number one going into Baltimore and Frankfurt, and people built for it. Then, Mega Garchomp showed up. 151 speed, pure dragon type, and a Draco Meteor that lands before Salamence gets to move. Joe UX9, who is that same Joseph Ugarte, says it plainly, "Garchomp C is what's keeping Salamence down." The results line up. Two top eights at each of four regionals, but none at Frankfurt and none at the latest one, Nice. &gt;&gt; But it's still everywhere on ladder. &gt;&gt; It is. On the Showdown ladder, it's the most used mega by a mile, on more than a quarter of teams. That's the gap. On ladder, Salamence still bullies people. At a regional, everyone has a plan for it. So, it's B tier. Still good, still a regional champion, just not the thing you build your whole team to beat anymore. Now for the 44 that never made it to a top table at all. &gt;&gt; F tier has one rule, zero published regional teams. Not low usage, none. Start with the Kanto classics. Mega Venusaur, Mega Charizard X, Mega Alakazam, Mega Slowbro, Mega Pinsir, Mega Gyarados. Mega Pinsir has Aerilate, the exact ability that made Salamence famous. Nobody took it to a regional. Mega Alakazam has 150 base speed, also nobody. Charizard X has Charizard Y right next to it. Exactly. To be fair, Joe UX9 thinks Venusaur and Charizard X can still sneak into day two. Neither has yet. Then the Megas with big numbers and no team. Mega Beedrill, Mega Medicham, Mega Heracross, Mega Sharpedo, Mega Glalie. Medicham's Pure Power doubles its attack. Glalie turns its normal moves into ice, doubling a stat isn't enough here. Next, the steel types. Mega Steelix, Mega Aggron, Mega Skarmory, Mega Scizor. Scizor's problem has a name. Mega Gallisopod is also bug and steel, and it just won a regional. Then the tricksters. Mega Sableye, Mega Banette, Mega Absol, Mega Malamar. Joe UX9 calls Sableye the number one cheese, and Banette with Prankster the lord of the cheese. At a regional, not once. The new Megas with strange abilities didn't make it either. Mega Golurk's contact moves go straight through protect. Mega Excadrill's do too, for a quarter of the damage. Mega Victreebel hurts you back when it faints. Mega Chimecho is psychic and steel now. Mega Electross gets Elevate. Elevate? Ground immunity plus a boost after a KO. It's a real ability. Also zero teams. And the rest of the pile. Mega Ampharos, Mega Feraligatr, Mega Houndoom, Mega Manectric, Mega Gallade, Mega Lucario, Mega Abomasnow, Mega Abominable, Mega Audino, Mega Scolipede, Mega Scrafty, Mega Chandelure, Mega Chesnaught, Mega Beartic, Mega Barbaracle, Mega Dragalge, Mega Hawlucha, Mega Crobat, Mega Drampa, Mega Falinks. That's 44 Megas, more than half the list in one row. Every one of them is legal and every one of them stayed home. D-tier is where it gets weird. One of them finished third at a regional. D-tier is a Mega that showed up, but only a handful of times across all six events. The weird one first. Mega female Meowstic finished third at Frankfurt. Then why is it D-tier? Because that team also carried Mega Charizard Y. It was on four teams in total. Joey 9's take, the team was good, not the Meowstic. Mega Slowbro gets huge power, which doubles its attack, and it still only showed up three times. Mega Mawile has the same ability, and its best run was ninth in Brisbane. Joey 9 has it a tier higher than we do, mostly on rain teams. Mega Lopunny, 13th in Baltimore. Mega Scovillain burns anything that hits it and got as high as 19th. Mega Absol Z is tied for the fastest Pokémon in the format at 151 speed. One team, 96th in Frankfurt. &gt;&gt; Fastest in the game, and it went 96th? &gt;&gt; Joey 9 files it somewhere between cheese and maybe day two, and says he's starting to believe in it. The results aren't there yet. Mega Sceptile is the strangest case. Joey 9 counted it among the most used Megas on day one of Nice, but it barely reached a published top list. Then the last few. Mega Kangaskhan, Mega Pidgeot, Mega Greninja, Mega Altaria, Mega Clefable. One or two teams each. Kangaskhan's parental bond makes every attack hit twice. Joey 9 called it really sad right now. And the results agree. 12 Megas, each with one or two good days and nothing to build on. C-tier is the opposite problem. Plenty of people use them, and they still can't make a top eight. &gt;&gt; Every Mega in C-tier has a real player base and zero top eight finishes. The biggest one is Mega Gardevoir. It's on 33 published teams, and it's one of the most used Megas on the ladder. It's best regional finish? Ninth in Recife. That many tries and not one top eight? JoeUX9's read, it's very linear. You have to build the whole team around it. And a Floatzel gives you fairy damage without spending your mega. Mega Aerodactyl is the other big one. 150 base speed. Tough claws. 23 published teams. Best finish? 11th. Here's where a pro disagrees with us. JoeUX9 puts Aerodactyl a tier higher because the Aerodactyl and Charizard teams keep doing well. The top eights just haven't come yet. Mega Blastoise has mega launcher which powers up pulse moves and a shell smash niche. Best finish? 17th. Mega Porygon-Z's fire mane boosts its fire moves by half again. Seven teams. Best finish? 18th. Mega Blaziken has speed boost in seven teams. Mega Lucario Z takes half damage from contact moves and has 151 speed. Nine teams. And Baxcalibur? Scraw summed it up before the first regional. It's Baxcalibur but bigger. Six teams. Best finish? 21st. Seven Megas that people believe in and that the results don't back up yet. B tier is where Megas start winning. One of them took a whole regional while everyone was looking at its twin. Every B tier Mega needs a specific team around it. Give it that team and it wins. Mega Raichu X won Nice. Cybertron pointed out that at the World Championships over a tenth of the field brought Raichu Y and not one player brought Raichu X. So it changed. Terrain. Rillaboom and the Indeedees arrived and Raichu X's electric surge overwrites their terrain the moment a Mega evolves. Electric terrain also means grounded Pokémon can't fall asleep which shuts down Venusaur's sleep powder. JoeUX9 calls it a toolbox Mega. The same rain team finished first and fourth at Nice. The week before Raichu X took third in Louisville on a different team. Mega Delphox is the opposite. Plenty of teams. Very few deep runs. 64 published teams. Only two top eights. Players found a trick around its own ability. Levitate means a Mega Delphox doesn't get boosted by Psychic Terrain. So, they fire Expanding Force before mega evolving. Mega Froslass sets Snow and Aurora Veil. It made the top 32 at five of the six regionals, but reached the top eight only twice. Mega Glalie made the top 32 at all six. The trick from Wolfey, let it take a physical hit first so Toxic Debris lays Toxic Spikes, then mega evolve into Adaptability. That sounds like a lot of setup. It is, and that's why it's B tier. It's best finish is seventh in Frankfurt. Mega Swampert is the rain cleaner with Swift Swim. Fourth in Louisville, fourth in Recife, eighth in Brisbane. But, Rillaboom's Grassy Glide hunts it, and when Charizard Y is everywhere, rain teams would rather bring a female Basculin. Mega Metagross took fourth in Baltimore with Tough Claws, then didn't make another top eight. Mega Camerupt has 20 base speed and Sheer Force, so it lives under Trick Room. On a Fairy Giraffe team, it took sixth in Nice. And plain Mega Garchomp, the one without the Z, it got slower when it mega evolves, but Sand Force powers up its ground, rock, and steel moves in sand. Last format, a Japanese player named Yanagi hit rank one on the ladder with a special Mega Garchomp next to Tyranitar. This format, it took third in Brisbane. Nine megas, each one great at one job, as long as you build that job. A tier is different. These megas don't need a special team. The game bends around them. A tier megas are the ones you have to plan for, whether or not you're using one. Mega Staraptor is the strangest Pokémon on this list. Its ability is Contrary, so every stat drop becomes a boost. Close Combat normally lowers your defenses. On Staraptor, it raises them, so every hit makes it tankier. Intimidate against it backfires, and Wolfey found the real trick. Whimsicott uses Charm on its own Staraptor, and Charm's attack drop becomes plus two. Charm your own partner? On purpose. It's on 76 teams with a top eight at three different regionals, and third at Nice. Mega Golisopod turns bug and steel when it mega evolves. That leaves one weakness, a four times weakness to fire, against eight resistances and a poison immunity. Tough claws works like a free life orb on its contact moves. The only real catch is base 40 speed. Second in Baltimore, second in Recife, and then at one Nice. It's also the second most used mega on the ladder. Mega Gengar shadow tag stops your opponent from switching out. Add perish song and you get a three turn clock they can't walk away from. Four top eights including second place at Nice. Joe UX9 says trapping is at its best right now because Rillaboom and Politoed give it so many good partners. There's always a catch. There is. Rain teams with Raichu X and a female Basculegion are a real problem for it. Mega Dragonite won the world championship before this format with Takuma Yamazaki. Multiscale halves the damage it takes at full health, which is why Cybertron calls it nearly impossible to one shot. This format it has five top eights from only 28 teams. No mega on 20 or more teams converts better. Mega Tyranitar is the last one. Sand stream sets the weather. Riptide gets a special defense boost in sand and dark type makes it immune to psychic moves. It won Baltimore next to Salamence and took third at Nice. Joe UX9's point, rain works without Raichu X, but sand doesn't work without Tyranitar. Five megas you can't ignore and still none of them is the best. S-tier has four megas. Between them, they've won four of the six regionals this format. S-tier means the format is built around you. Every team either uses you or has a plan for you. Mega Eternal Flower Floette has more top eight finishes than any other mega in the game at all six regionals. Fairy Aura is Xerneas' signature ability. It powers up every fairy move on the field and Floette's own Light of Ruin hits for 140. But Reddit says it fell off. Players on r/vgc say it fell off in this format. Meanwhile, it made a top eight at every single regional. The catch is it has never won one. Second in Brisbane is its best finish. Mega Raichu Y is the most used mega at regionals on about a quarter of all published teams. No guard means none of its moves can miss. Zap Cannon normally hits half the time and it always paralyzes. So every Zap Cannon is a guaranteed paralysis. Players on r/VGC put it best. You can stall out tailwind, but you can't stall out paralysis. It won Frankfurt with Eric Rios sharing the team with the next mega on the list. Before the first regional, Scross had Mega Garchomp Z wasn't winning one. It has won two. First and second at Frankfurt both had it. It won Brisbane and it was second again at Nice. Just because it's fast, because of who it's fast against. 151 speed is faster than Raichu Y, Salamence, and Charizard Y. Draco Meteor handles Salamence and Power Gem handles Charizard before either of the moves. JoeUX9 says it's the check that keeps the other top megas honest, which leaves one mega. The one this format was supposed to move past, Mega Charizard Y. Drought sets sun the moment it mega evolves and every fire move it throws gets the sun boost. It's on 133 published teams with 12 top eights. It won three of the six regionals, more than any other mega. Two of those wins came with Charizard as the only mega on the team. JoeUX9 says it's one of the only megas that can carry a team by itself. Didn't this format start without it? In usage, yes. JoeUX9 pointed out it wasn't even among the top 12 Pokémon on day one of Baltimore. Players on r/VGC say it came back because the things that answered it fell away. Then the Louisville final was Charizard Y against Charizard Y. JoeUX9 won it. So here's the board. Four S tiers, five A tiers, nine B tiers, and 44 megas that never left the house. One question this list can't settle. Flouret has more top eights than anything and no trophy. Is that S tier or the best runner up in the game?
