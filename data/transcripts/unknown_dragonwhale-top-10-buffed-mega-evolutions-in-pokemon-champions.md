# Top 10 BUFFED Mega Evolutions in Pokemon Champions

So, the 2025 Pokemon World Championships concluded, and the first regulations of Pokemon Champions has been announced. The first battles in this game will involve Mega Evolutions, which we get to use thanks to a special item called an Omni Ring. The return of Mega Evolutions next year marks 6 years since we were last playing with them in Ultra Sun and Moon. In this video, I will be showcasing the top 10 megas that received the most buffs during these past six years. In this video, we will be ignoring side games like Let's Go Pikachu Eevee, Legends Arus, and BDSP, as these games are a little weird with move sets. We start off the list with number 10, which is going to be Mega Gardevore. Any psychic fair's worst enemy is the steel type, which is why Gardevore often ran Focus Blast in generation 7. However, now Gardevoir has access to mystical fire, which is a fantastic and consistent coverage move for it. Psychic noise is also a new option for a psychic move. While weaker than psychic will disrupt opponents who are trying to stall out guard, guard also newly learned vacuum wave, which is a special priority move. Thanks to this, it no longer has to rely on shadow sneak to take out focus stash Pokémon. All in all, Mega Gardavore received nice additions, but none that were too game-changing, placing it last on this list. Up next at number nine, we have Mega Alaria. Alaria was not exactly a strong mega evolution in generation 7. It has faced some downgrades in the past six years. For one, Ruse's PP has been decreased to five, meaning that Alaria cannot spend too many turns setting up and healing at the same time. Physical mega Alaria sets also no longer have access to its most consistent powerful move, return and frustration. However, Alaria makes this list thanks to a few additions to its move pool, namely fire spin, willowisp, and body slam. Fire spin is a terrifying addition to Altaria for Pokemon lacking offensive pressure, as Alaria can trap it and perisong while the opponent is unable to take it out within the three- turn limit. Willowisp and body slam are nice moves for Alaria to spread status, making it even more annoying to deal with. While the nerfs certainly hurt Mega Alaria, the buffs were enough to put it in ninth place on this list. Pushing onward to eighth place, we have Mega Sabali. Mega Sabali is a very defensive mega evolution, and it already had a lot of good status moves, but somehow it got even more in the past two generations. For starters, it has gained encore, meaning that prior to Mega Evolving, Sable Eye has the option to lock opposing Pokémon with Prankster if they chose to set up. While Willowis usually has a lot more utility for Sable Eye, it has also gained Thunderwave, which allows it to potentially cheese out a few free turns using its longevity. On the topic of inflicting status, Sabalai also gained hex, which can let Sabai actually dish out decent damage against status opponents, especially after some calm line boosts. While Sabilai's versatility has certainly gone up with new available options, the most important moves for Sabilite is Recover. due to recovers nerf to 5 PP in Pokemon champions. The downside is way too heavy to play Sable Eye higher than eighth place. Moving on to number seven, we have Mega Obama Snow. Abama Snow is one of two Pokémon in this list where it got buffed in areas outside of the move pool. In generation 9, ice types were buffed to increase their defense by 50% under snow, which Mega Obama Snow activates with its snow warning ability. On top of that, Obama Snow now has Aurora Veil, allowing it to become even tankier, or at the very least set up screens in elasticish effort for its teammates. One of Obama Snow's main downside is that it often needs to run a mix attacker set if it wants to run its strongest move, Blizzard, as well as moves to hit steel types, Earthquake. However, Abom Snow now gets access to earth power, allowing Abom Snow to fully invest in special attack without neglecting coverage for steel types. While these buffs are not enough to put Obama snow amongst the top tier mega evolutions, it is certainly a good step forward and earns the seventh slot on this list. Up next in number six, we have Mega Lony. Following the Bama Snow, Lony is the second of the two Pokémon in this list to get buffs outside of its move pool. Mega Lopony scrappy ability received a buff in generation 8 to also ignore intimidate attack drops, which is huge, especially in VGC. Lony now has access to close combat which is while weaker than high jump kick is way less risky. A major move addition is triple axle which while not exactly consistent is way more powerful than the ice punch that Lony had to use as coverage back in generation 7. It also got U-turn which is very useful tool for Lony to pivot and gain control of the battle. With all these great additions to the move pull and ability, Lony would have been higher on this list if it weren't for the huge hit it took when it lost return and frustration. Now, you might think you can run double edge, but it doesn't even get that in Sword and Shield. However, it did get this move in Legends Arus, which we decided to ignore for this video. So, it's still up in the air whether or not it can have it in Pokemon Champions. Taking into account the ups and downs for this Pokemon, it has been placed into sixth place on this list. We are halfway through the list as we enter into fifth place. Mega Charizard Y being a special fire type that outdamages Chiu, Mega Charizard Y became even stronger in generation 9 thanks to Weather Ball. While slightly weaker than Fire Blast, Weather Ball is 100% accurate and stronger than the equally accurate flamethrower. Charizard also got access to Scorching Sands, which is an amazing move to have against Heatran and Tyrantar, which otherwise easily switches into Charizard. Finally, Charizard also gets access to a powerful special flying type attack with Hurricane, although it has negative synergy with the harsh sunlight. However, not everything is looking up for Charizard Y as it has lost access to hidden power, which it used in generation 7 to threaten opposing Salamaments. With Weather Ball, Scorching Sands, and Solar Beam dealing little damage against Salamaments, many Charizard Y may opt to run Dragon Pulse in Pokemon Champions. Taking this into consideration, Mega Charizard Y still has gained a significant boost and earned its place in number five. Up next in fourth place, we have another top tier mega, Mega Metagross. Mega Metagross probably gained the best steel type move it could have asked for, Heavy Slam. Mega Metagross is the sixth heaviest Pokemon of all time, and Heavy Slam deals 120 base power damage if the Pokemon is over four times heavier than the target. To put this into perspective, even the newlyannounced Mega Victory Bell is getting hit with a full power heavy slam. On the psychic side, Metagross also gains psychic fangs, which is both more powerful and accurate than Zen Headbutt that it used in generation 7. On the coverage side, Metagross gained knockoff, which is always a useful move to have. All in all, Metagross gained a massive boost to its stab options and didn't even lose anything relevant. However, it's still fourth on this list because the buffs the top three Pokémon have received are even bigger than this. The first of the top three is Mega Bayet, landing in third place. Mega Bayet is one of the weakest Mega Evolutions in generation 7, but over the past 6 years, this Pokémon was blessed with new moves. In terms of offense, Bayet got access to the newly added Poltergeist, which elevates damage output significantly compared to Shadowclaw. Bayonet also got Encore, which with its prankster ability can lock down any opponent that goes for a status move against it, as long as they aren't a dark type. If you want to use Mega Bayet as a sweeper, you also now have the option to use Sword Stance, which pairs well with Shadow Sneak to make Mega Bayet a threat to reckon with. In summary, Mega Bayet got a significant boost to its viability, earning its podium placement on this list. We're almost finished as we get into second place, Mega Gengar. So, I'm going to be completely honest with everyone here. I was debating for a long time whether to put this Pokemon in first place due to how this one particular buff fundamentally and completely breaks the game. The only reason why I put Mega Gengar in second place instead of first is because, to be honest, it was already bordering on breaking the fundamentals of the game in generation 7, so the jump isn't as absurd. The one buff that I'm talking about for Mega Gengar is Encore. If this Pokemon encores you into a move that you don't want to be locked into, the game pretty much just ends at that instant. You can't switch out. You're locked into a bad move and the Gengar is able to walk all over you. This is exacerbated even further now that Gengar has nasty plot, meaning it can boost to plus six while the opponent is locked into their predicament. This phenomenon also applies to VGC as clicking Fake out when the opponent has a Gengar is pretty much a self-inflicted death sentence. In the following turn, you will have a Pokemon locked into Fake Out and unable to switch, meaning you will be fighting a 2v1 battle until the Gengar is knocked out. Honestly, it wouldn't surprise me if Gengar loses Encore in Pokemon champions because this Pokemon will actually be a little too ridiculous to let loose on both singles and doubles formats. Finally, we are at the first place Mega Evolution you've been waiting for or skipped the video to Mega Blastoise. Similar to Mega Gengar, Mega Blastoise gained a significant boost to its viability thanks to a single move. The move in question is Shell Smash, a move that most competitive Pokemon players agree is the best setup move in the game. While Blastoise did have shell smash since Sword and Shield, it never really had the stats to break into the metag game during the past two generations. However, things are very different with Mega Blastoise. The special attack stat is increased dramatically, and the extra bulk makes it even harder to knock out before it sets up. Mega Blastoise will most likely become the best shell smash user in Pokemon Champions and a mega evolution that everyone will need to prepare against. In the past, Mega Blastoise was considered to be way weaker than the other KTO starter mega evolutions, but now it can be argued to be the strongest of them all. Despite gaining nothing else worth of note, the surge in viability from Shell Smash alone is so drastic that it has earned Mega Blastoise the number one spot on this list. And there you have it.
