Welcome back to Zero to Hero. Today we're back with the How to Actually Play series where we take a look at a specific archetype, move, or Pokemon, and look at the specifics on both how to play and how to play against it. Today we're looking at the Setup archetype, which also includes the Protect the King archetype. Just before we get into it, make sure to leave a like and subscribe, and comment which archetype you want to see considered next. Let's take a look at Setup first, then consider what exactly Protect the King is. A Setup team has one or more Pokemon with powerful Setup moves, and relies on those Pokemon to increase their stats, specifically their damage, to then take over a game. The most common Setup moves are things like Nasty Plot which doubles Special Attack, Quiver Dance which increases Special Attack Spadeff and Speed by one stage, or even things like Calm Mind which increase Special Attack and Special Defense by one stage each. Special Attacking Setup Pokemon tend to be better for this role, as they won't be slowed down by the very prevalent Intimidate. However there are other viable physical options, like Swords Dance and Dragon Dance, but again Special Attackers tend to be stronger Setup Pokemon. Another key aspect of having a strong Setup Pokemon is that Pokemon having a solid Spread move, as a Pokemon can set up all they want, but if they can only ever deal with one of your opponent's Pokemon at a time, their sweep will take much longer, giving your opponent more opportunities to overcome them. And again, most powerful Spread moves tend to be special, making Special Attacking Setup Pokemon tend to be better. The first team we'll look at comes from Regulation MC and focuses on Volcarona. However it does include two Pokemon that can set up. Volcarona here has one of the premier Setup moves in Quiver Dance, whereas Floette, the other Setup Pokemon, has Calm Mind. And again, both are powerful Special Setup sweepers with strong Spread attacks. This team also includes one of the strongest two tools to enable a Pokemon to set up, Fake Out. This team has not one, not two, but three Fake Out users in Incineroar, Rillaboom, and Raichu. Fake Out has such good synergy with Setup, partially because it forces, protects, or switches from the opponent, which is a very passive turn, and if the opponent is protecting or switching to try and avoid getting faked out, that means they aren't attacking, giving you a perfect opportunity to get a Setup move off on your sweeper without it taking damage. Volcarona here also has more Setup than usual, with Grassy Seed, which will activate off of Rillaboom switching in in its Grassy Terrain, giving Volcarona a defense boost, increasing all of its relevant stats after a Quiver Dance, and while increasing its physical and special defense, it makes it very difficult to remove. Not to mention the benefit that Grassy Terrain adds to Volcarona or Floette, giving some passive healing, really lets them stick around longer to either get more Setup moves off, or get off more attacks to finish a game. Immediate Offense from Basculegion or Raichu here also increases defensive play from your opponent, which again gives you room to setup. Taking a look at a second Regulation MC team, this one has Goldengo and Primarina that would like to setup, using Nasty Plot and Calm Mind respectively. This team has only 2 Fake Out Pokemon in Rillaboom and Raichu, which is more than sufficient as 3 Fake Out Pokemon is quite a bit, and these Setup Pokemon do not have a way to increase their speed like Volcarona did, and they're not as fast as something like Floette, so instead this team tends to be more of a bulky Setup team rather than a fast one. Primarina here is also holding the Leftovers, and Goldengo has amazing defensive typing. This team also has a bit more Immediate Offense than the Volcarona team does without needing to setup. Life War 1 Goldengo also allows it to hit very hard, and it has other strong offensive options right away, again like Raichu and something like Arcanine here. The team does function similarly with 2 potential Setup Pokemon, and being able to pick which one is better for any given matchup. It still includes Fake Out partners, has grassy terrain, and Leftovers for more recovery on the Setup Pokemon, and again a strong physical threat to balance the heavy special damage in Arcanine. Staraptor however on this team is also a setup Pokemon. With Close Combat and Contrary, it is able to spam Close Combat while increasing both its defenses, further contributing to the bulkier playstyle of this team. When Mega Staraptor first entered the fray, it did start off more as a Protect the King style team, which we will consider now, and how it's a bit different from just general setup. Protect the King is a term that comes from Chess, as if you lose your King in chess, you lose the game. So a Protect your King style team is banking your entire game plan on one powerful Pokemon, making it your King, whereas if you lose that Pokemon you will pretty much lose the game. They are generally more of a rare team archetype due to the higher risk of needing to keep one Pokemon safe, rather than being able to rely on your whole team to dish out damage. Again Staraptor teams started off with this, pairing it with Whimsicott to boost Staraptor's stats with Charm or Tickle, then added something like Sinistia to heal Staraptor while providing Redirection support, and they often ran Grimmsnarl also getting screens making Staraptor incredibly bulky. You can see how none of these three Pokemon are really threatening any damage, but also how powerful Staraptor is with all of that setup. We'll also look at an example from Regulation I that showcases the Protect the King style team further. Restricted formats tend to see the most Protect the King style teams due to the overwhelming power of Restricted Pokemon, as it makes sense to use them as a King piece rather than other regular Pokemon in typical formats since their raw power eclipses that of a regular Pokemon. Kylie Rex Shadow Rider was the premier King piece due to its powerful spread attack, incredible stats, typing that's immune to Fake Out, and access to both Nasty Plot and Calm Mind. There are two sets it would tend to run, one being Astral Barrage, Psychic, Nasty Plot and Protect, or the other being Astral Barrage, Draining Kiss, Calm Mind and Protect with Terrifairy. It would take advantage of two Fake Out users in Incineroar and Rillaboom, while also benefiting again from the grassy terrain Rillaboom provides, and would often use Clefairy not only for good redirection that resists dark types that Kylie Rex is weak to, but it also has Friend Guard, boosting Kylie Rex's survivability even further. Clefairy also had access to Life Due and Helping Hand, which could further heal Kylie Rex or pick up one-shots on things that resisted Kylie Rex's attacks thanks to both a setup and a Helping Hand boost. Some teams would also run two redirectors in both Clefairy and Indeedee, as Indeedee would be able to redirect the ghost type attacks that Kylie Rex is weak to while being immune to it, and also being able to support with Helping Hand, while blocking opposing priority moves thanks to the Psychic Drain. So what are some specific tactics you can use with setup teams? They've already mostly been discussed, but we'll just break them down a little bit further. The first one is Fake Out Cycling, and this isn't necessarily specific to setup teams, but it is very effective on them. Leading a setup Pokemon next to a Fake Out user lets you click Fake Out, forcing one opposing Pokemon to not move, keeping your setup Pokemon safe. And again, the pressure that Fake Out exerts also incentivizes your opponent to protect with both Pokemon or make a switch, which again is a very defensive turn, allowing your setup Pokemon to get off its setup move without taking any damage. After you get your Fake Out and setup, you can simply protect your setup Pokemon while switching into your second Fake Out user, letting you do the same thing the next turn, getting a setup move and clicking Fake Out. Very basic strategy, but quite effective. The second is using redirectors. We didn't really see many redirectors on the Regulation MC teams, partially due to the limited Pokedex of the format, but they are very useful. And they're also very good when used in conjunction with Fake Out users, as you can use your Fake Out user on turn one, get a setup move off, and then switch in your redirector if your opponent has only single target damage on the field to be able to redirect those moves and get off another setup move. The last one here is more of a specific tactic, and that's the timing of getting off a setup move in the mid or end game. A perfect opportunity to set up is when you have a form of speed control that is ending, like Tailwind or Trick Room. This is used more on teams that aren't as heavy setup focused, but have it as an option. For example, you could have one turn of Tailwind left. Your opponent is quite incentivized, again to protect both Pokemon, to stall out the last turn of Tailwind. However, if your Pokemon with a setup move is on the field and poised to set up, that can be a crucial turn where your opponent is protecting both Pokemon, and you can easily get that setup move off, allowing you to continue your sweep after your speed control ends. So how do we beat setup teams? There is a reason that this is a less used archetype, as there is quite a bit of counterplay to these teams, especially if you have some options built into your team, and have the wherewithal to identify opposing setup teams before the game starts. The first most obvious counter is spread moves, as spread moves will ignore redirection from an opponent. Being able to hit both of your opponent's Pokemon while one is setting up and one is trying to redirect, completely negates their redirection, basically making it a 2v1 if you have two Pokemon with spread moves. Next is using speed control. If your opponent is trying to boost their speed with something like Quiver Dance Volcarona, if you have a trick room game plan, you can trick room very early, essentially making them weaker after they set up, making them faster than things they might not otherwise be faster than, or you can use those to take them out. Using your own Tailwind is also a good option, as many setup Pokemon don't have a way to increase their speed. The aforementioned Goldengo, Primarina, Floette, and Calyrex all are not increasing their speed, so while they may be increasing their damage and or their bulk, if you can still maintain speed control in the match, you will be able to take them out. There are many anti-setup tools, things like Intimidate for physical attackers. Snarl is a very powerful option against special setup Pokemon, being able to reduce their special setup and essentially negate their entire use of the move, and things like Icy Wind can also slow down ones that want to speed up. Having a Focus Sash on your team is also an incredible option against setup Pokemon. The entire point of a setup Pokemon is to be able to pick up one-hit knockouts, whereas if you have a Pokemon with a Focus Sash, it doesn't matter how much setup they have, it will always be able to survive one attack of theirs and respond with something in kind. Being able to switch it in on the right time, either to tank an attack and then outspeed with a move afterwards, or letting it get a switch in, get an attack off, then go down to its Focus Sash and get a second attack, might be what you need. Next up is incredibly smart switching. There are some setup Pokemon that have very limited offensive pressure. For example, Calyrex Shadow Rider tends to run only Psychic and Ghost moves, both of which are resisted by Dark. So if you have a strong Dark type, you can switch in on one of these attacks and eat the hit. Another Pokemon that was not necessarily a setup Pokemon but had a similar defensive counterplay was Ursuluna. It had access to both Facade and Headlong Rush, which were Normal and Ground moves. If you find yourself in a situation against a Pokemon like that, where you need to be able to switch in safely and not get taken out by it, you can bring in a Ghost type when you're expecting it to use Facade, and then you can bring in a Flying type when you're expecting it to use Headlong Rush. Sometimes you're able to juggle good switches like this and completely negate any damage from these powerful Pokemon, while your partner is able to rack up damage on them. And our last and most obvious form of counterplay against setup Pokemon is to just overwhelm them before they can get set up. Sometimes this is difficult if your opponent has many defensive tools like Fake Out and Redirection. But perhaps don't double protect in front of a board state that has a Fake Out Pokemon and a setup one. Just go for attacks with both of your Pokemon. Or try to lead Pokemon that are immune to Fake Out and are able to guaranteed get that damage off right away. Although you may not be able to remove the setup Pokemon right away, you will be able to hit it pretty hard, allowing you to clean it up, perhaps with priority moves later on. Again, this can be harder if they have options like Raichu or Calyrex Shadow Rider that could just go for strong immediate damage right away, creating a bit of a 50-50 mind game if you should protect to avoid the Fake Out, if you should protect to avoid the strong damage, or just attack and try and get through the Fake Out.
