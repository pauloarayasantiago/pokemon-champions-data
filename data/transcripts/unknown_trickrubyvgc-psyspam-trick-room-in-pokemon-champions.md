# Psyspam Trick Room in Pokemon Champions!

**Channel:** TrickRubyVGC | **Date:** unknown | **Views:** 2,742 | **Duration:** 35:02

---

Pokemon Champions is here, and sadly, many of my favourite Pokemon are not. Pokemon Champions brings us a large change in refresh to which Pokemon are allowed to be brought to competitive play. I can understand why certain Pokemon, like Ursuluna or Indeedee may be omitted, but then again, who invited this guy? One would be reasonable to assume that without Indeedee or another Psychic Surge Pokemon, that Seisman must be dead in this game, yes? Well, no. I don't give up easily, and I really wanted to figure out a way to get the titles for winning games with Armor Rouge. So, I decided to see if Seisman could be done using a manual Psychic Terrain setter, and we landed on male Meowstic. The general idea to make Seisman work in this format is to get Trick Room up after leading with Meowstic and an Expanding Force user, then on the first turn of Trick Room, you set up Psychic Terrain with Meowstic and start blasting with a partner. For this team, I decided on three different Expanding Force users for different purposes. Armor Rouge is included not just because he's my special spicy boy, but because Hatterene would struggle a bit harder into all of the king gamuts going around, so having the fire damage helps there. I also opted for Mega Slowbro as both are Mega and primary Expanding Force users. Slowbro works well with Meowstic to get Trick Room up by being immune to Taunt. If the opponent has another way to deal with Slowbro, you can also opt to Trick Room with Meowstic since he's got a Focus Sash and is unlikely to be taken out in one turn. Free Graph originally had Twin Beam, but by the time I was recording this video, I did switch to Expanding Force. Let me go over the team's makeup in a bit more detail, and then I'll demonstrate how it works. Despite not being the biggest damage dealer, Meowstic is technically the most crucial Pokemon on the team. Without him, we cannot size-pam at all. The moveset is fairly straightforward, and I don't think there's any room for variation. Fake Out is necessary for getting Trick Room up safely in a lot of matchups, and while it doesn't come up as often, having Meowstic running Trick Room allows for more existence in setup and opening lines that the team may otherwise lack. Psychic Terrain and Expanding Force round out the set and speak for themselves. Since Meowstic is not performing a long-lasting role in battle, I often go for maximum special attack. I originally had Speed investment as well, but decided to train my Meowstic in HP instead so he's still slow enough to be useful in Trick Room. Even with the speed of reducing nature, Meowstic will still outspeed almost every Incineroar or Fake Out, so you can feel safe to get Trick Room up in front of the Fire Kitty. Despite the HP investment, I still have Meowstic carry a Focus Sash, so that he almost always survives Turn 1 in order to get Sad Group Terrain up after. Slow Bro's Taunt immunity before it Mega Evolves allows us to generally get Trick Room up much more easily, without having Psychic Surge redirection available. Meowstic can handle most Fake Outs with its own, so Slow Bro only has to worry about super-effective hits, and Sneezler's Fake Out. If Oblivious is not needed to block Taunt, you can Mega Evolve on Turn 1 to make use of the extra bulk. I've yet to see Slow Bro drop to a super-effective physical hit with that 180 base defense, but you do need to be careful with special damage. With a base defense that high, Mega Slow Bro can dish out fearsome body pressures without really needing extra investment, allowing you to put all the rest of your stats into HP and special attack. While not as obscene as its defense, Slow Bro's special attack is very serviceable for clearing opponents in this format with expanding force. I'd be lying if I didn't say Armarouge was the reason I built this team. As one of my all-time favorite Pokemon and a mascot of the channel, I had to find a way to make Spicy Bro work. But without a DD, power herb, or meteor beam, or even weakness policy for beat-up shenanigans, I was really struggling to fit him somewhere. As it turned out, this Mega Slow Bro team was perfect for him. Acting as the second main center of Trick Room after Slow Bro, Armarouge goes well with Meowstic when Slow Bro's offensive or defensive profile is not as suitable to your needs. Hatterene was also considered for this slot, but Armarouge's Fire Staff and Fire immunity for Flash Fire both made him a fair bit more helpful for this role. Like most Trick Room sweepers, on Armarouge you offer maximum defensive training into HP and special attack, with the last little bit in special defense. For the moves, you could consider Wide Yard over Protect, but for this particular team, I have not found Wide Yard to be needed. When Fighting-type coverage was needed, Glade felt like a perfect fit both for setting Trick Room on its own when needed, as well as for having further synergy with Psychic Terrain. Between its sharpness ability and the damage boost from Psychic Terrain, Glade's Psycho Cut is incredibly hard-hitting, and is one of the strongest neutral moves on the team, often critting thanks to the equipped scope lens. Sacred Sword is the fighting move of choice on all Pokemon that can run it, since it ignores the opponent's defense and evasion boosts. In a format of Archer Ludon, this is very, very welcome. For the last move, I was again considering Wide Guard, but opted to add Night Slash instead, despite already having Kin Gambit on the team. I don't always bring Kin Gambit into matchups where I'd otherwise like a dark move, and Night Slash helps to cover for that. For stats, I looked into if it was meaningfully possible to give Glade certain survivability with more Physical Bolt, but I didn't find any worthwhile calcs in the current format, so I opted to stick with the usual Max Max. Being the only non-Psychic-type on the team, Kin Gambit helps to tie up our matchups into opposing Psychic types very well. It also acts as an excellent deterrent to bringing in Cineroar, and allows us to set up options next to it for Rigorath. In this version of the team, I opted for Kowtow Cleave, Sucker Punch, Iron Head, and Swords Dance, but I've also played around with Low Kick and Protect in this place of Swords Dance. Chaffle Berry helps Kin Gambit be a more consistent lead against Sneezler when paired with Rigorath. Because this Kin Gambit is more often operating in Trick Room and not trying to live as many hits, I prefer to go for the max possible attack to get the cleanest 180 KOs. However, you could also opt to go a little more investment into Special Defense, since Kin Gambit has such high attack as is. Friggurath fills the most niche role on the team. It is pretty much only brought when Fake Out needs to be blocked by it, and since Meowstic already generally handles the Cineroar Fake Outs or the Rare Blastoise one, Friggurath is mostly just for Sneezler. Friggurath is typically led with Meowstic or Kin Gambit, depending on how soft the opposing team is to expanding force. If you want to be playing Seismic Game Plans, you lead Meowstic with Friggurath and essentially treat it like you would leading Meowstic in Slowbro. However, because Friggurath is so bulky, you're also sometimes getting away with using Trick Room and Psychic Terrain on Turn 1, giving you more immediate firepower during Trick Room. If you're really lucky in Closed Team Sheet, you may find opponents that double into Meowstic on turns like this, giving you a free switch into a bigger and slower threat. For stats, I decided to go for a little more Special Attack on Friggurath, since it is spamming Hyper Voice and Expanding Force, but if you find that it's not surviving and getting Trick Room up as safely as you often need it to, you could opt for a bit more bulk to make up for the difference.
