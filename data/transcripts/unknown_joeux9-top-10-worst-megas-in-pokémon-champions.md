# Top 10 Worst Megas In Pokémon Champions

**Channel:** JoeUX9 | **Date:** unknown | **Views:** 6,686 | **Duration:** 16:47

---

Some mega evolutions are absolutely terrifying in Pokémon Champions. Mega Charizard Y can take over games in just a few turns with drought and powerful sun-boosted moves. Mega Floette hits like a truck with fairy aura light of ruin and is very flexible in the format. Mega Gengar can make you question every decision you've ever made after you lose the perish trap. And then there are the mega Pokémon that make you wonder who approved this. Today we're counting down the 10 worst mega evolutions in Pokémon Champions. These are the megas that are completely outclassed or just straight awful compared to the other mega evolutions in the game. Kicking off the list is Mega Gallade. Now, since Mega Gallade is our number 10 spot, I wouldn't exactly say it's unusable. In fact, I actually think it's slightly better than the megas we'll get to later on in the list. That being said, Mega Gallade still has some major issues, and it's not just the fact that it can't farm aura like Agamemnon in The Odyssey. For starters, its psychic and fighting typing leaves it with a long list of weaknesses, making it very difficult to position safely. Combined with its mediocre bulk, it becomes very vulnerable to many common attacks, meaning it struggles to stand the field against the format's strongest Pokémon. Its mega ability isn't doing it favors, either. Mega Gallade gains inner focus, but that's honestly a downgrade from what regular Gallade offers with sharpness. Sharpness boosts several of regular Gallade's best attacks, primarily sacred sword and psycho cut, which means that regular Gallade noticeably exerts more offensive pressure while also having an item available. On top of that, because you don't have inner focus before mega evolution, Gallade can still be hit by intimidate before it mega evolves, making it much harder to take advantage of its increased attack stat. We also need to talk about its speed tier. Mega Gallade is definitely fast, creeping above common Pokémon like Sneasler, Mega Charizard Y, and Garchomp, but it's in one of those awkward speed tiers where it's fast but not quite fast enough. It still falls short of many of the format's premier threats while speed tying with Mega Sceptile and Mega Metagross, meaning often needs a lot of additional support to get anything done. The main saving grace that gives Mega Gallade a niche is the option of a fast stone edge to snipe Mega Charizard Y, triple axel to snipe Garchomp along with focus sash Venusaur, and a generally powerful close combat that can output good damage in the right spot. Not to mention it has access to a wide range of other coverage moves it can exploit as well. This means that Mega Gallade ends up in an awkward spot. It's not completely inviable by any means, but compared to the other Mega Evolutions in the meta, it just doesn't really stand out. And trust me, the Megas only get worse from here, especially that number one spot. Coming in at number nine is Mega Heracross. And just like Mega Gallade, this isn't a Pokémon without strengths. In fact, Mega Heracross has one thing going for it immediately. It's absolutely massive attack stat. Pairing that with solid natural bulk means it can take hits while threatening to KO just about anything that doesn't resist to the attacks. It also looks like the kind of bug that sneaks into my room every summer. Not very fun, I'll tell you that much. It's speed also isn't completely unusable, at least being slow enough that you can consider using it as a Trick Room sweeper. The problem is its speed tier is just awkward enough to make certain situations very uncomfortable. It's still moving after a lot of Pokémon you actually want to underspeed in Trick Room. This includes non-speed invested Incineroar and Sylveon, which are fairly common in the format. This makes it much less reliable than other dedicated Trick Room sweepers that can consistently underspeed those threats. Offensively though, Mega Heracross definitely packs a punch. Close Combat hits incredibly hard, and thanks to Skill Link, moves like Pin Missile and Rock Blast always connect four or five times, which gives it the ability to break through Pokémon with Focus Sash, Multiscale, or Sturdy, assuming it doesn't get repeatedly hit by Intimidate first. Unfortunately, it's now time to talk about its biggest problem. Mega Heracross's Bug and Fighting typing leaves it vulnerable to a long list of common attacking types like Fire, Fairy, and Flying, which means it struggles against many of the format's biggest threats. Pokémon like Mega Floette, Charizard Y, and Mega Sceptile all threaten Mega Heracross, making it difficult to position effectively throughout the games you bring it in. When you combine its awkward speed tier with its poor defensive typing, Mega Heracross ends up needing a lot of support and precise positioning to become the star it was meant to be. The raw power is definitely there, but compared to the Mega Evolutions ranked above it, it simply has too many hurdles to make investing in it seriously worth it. At number eight, we have Mega Sharpedo. Mega Sharpedo could see some use on ranked battles, but it's generally something you'd want to stay away from in a serious competitive setting. Before mega evolving, it gets speed boost, which means you can protect on the first turn to pick up a plus one in its speed stat. You can then mega evolve at the following turn, which gives it strong jaw. And that turns biting moves like crunch, psychic fangs, and ice fang into legitimately threatening attacks. Crunch is great against Pokémon like Basculegion. Psychic fangs lets it pressure Sneasler. Its water type attacks are excellent into Incineroar. And ice fang gives it a way to threaten Garchomp. Thanks to the speed boost, it's also fast enough to outspeed all those Pokémon after mega evolving. The problem is Mega Sharpedo is incredibly frail and misses out on a lot of knockouts. Even though it's usually moving first, it often can't afford to take the average attack in the format. That makes positioning Mega Sharpedo safely a serious challenge throughout an entire game. And even when it does get into favorable positions, the damage can still feel really underwhelming. It does a great job picking off the Pokémon it's specifically designed to beat, but against neutral targets, it often falls short of the knockouts you'd expect from a mega evolution. On top of that, some of the format's biggest threats are still a huge problem. Mega Charizard Y and Mega Florges are both difficult match-ups, while Incineroar can consistently cycle intimidate, dropping Mega Sharpedo's damage output even further. So, while the speed boost and the strong jaw combo is definitely cool, it just isn't enough to carry Mega Sharpedo to real success beyond cheesing people on ranked battles. Between its paper-thin defenses, reliance on favorable positioning, and stiff competition from other mega evolutions, Mega Sharpedo lands at number eight. Not the worst, but definitely not great.
