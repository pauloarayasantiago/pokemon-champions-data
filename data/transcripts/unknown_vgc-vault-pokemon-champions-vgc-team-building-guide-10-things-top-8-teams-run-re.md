# Pokemon Champions VGC Team Building Guide: 10 Things Top 8 Teams Run (Reg M-C)

**Channel:** VGC Vault | **Date:** unknown | **Views:** 491 | **Duration:** 9:08

---

We pulled every top-eight team from all eight regionals this format, 64 in all, counting a list again each time it made another top eight, even in different hands, and checked what they have in common. Ten things kept showing up. So what's on all of them? Number one is on 63 of the 64. The one team without it still finished third at Baltimore. And one ingredient is only on 38 top-eight teams, but seven of the eight regional winners ran it. The ranking is simple — how many of the 64 top-eight teams run it, with regional wins breaking ties. Where it tells us something, we also compare against all 824 published team lists to show what top teams did more and what they did less, starting at number 10. Number 10 is a terrain setter, a Pokémon whose ability sets a terrain when it switches in, or for Mega Raichu X, when it Mega Evolves. Thirty-six of the 64 top-eight teams ran one, and 28 of those were the same Pokémon, Rillaboom with Grassy Surge. Grassy Terrain is what turns on Grassy Glide. It only gets plus one priority when the user is on Grassy Terrain, so Rillaboom brings its own. One R slash Pokémon Champions player complained that Rillaboom gets both Fake Out and Priority Grassy Glide. All 28 of those top-eight Rillaboom ran Fake Out. So why is it only number 10? Because 36 is the lowest count on this list, and the next entry ties it, but one more. It also showed up less at the top. Almost two-thirds of all published lists had a terrain setter, against just over half of the top eight. It still won three regionals, with three different terrains. Psychic Terrain from Indeedee at Baltimore, Grassy at Frankfurt, and Electric Terrain from Mega Raichu X at Nice. Number 9 is a pivot move. Here, that means one of four moves that hit or weaken the target and then switch the user out. Parting Shot, U-Turn, Volt Switch, or Flip Turn. It's also on 36 top-eight teams, the same as Terrain, but it won five regionals to Terrain's three, so it ranks higher. Inceneroar is the reason. It carried Parting Shot on 24 of those teams. Parting Shot lowers the target's attack and special attack by one stage. Then Inceneroar switches out. Why would you want to leave? Because Fake Out only works on a Pokémon's first turn out, and Intimidate triggers every time it switches in. Leave, come back later, and you get both again. One R slash VGC player running Rillaboom and Inceneroar put it simply. Fake Out, Cycling, and Intimidate options. Behind Inceneroar, Rillaboom ran U-Turn on eight top-eight teams, and Grimmsnarl ran Parting Shot on seven. Number eight is the first item on the list. Citrus Berry. When the holder drops to half its HP or below, it eats the berry and restores a quarter of its max HP. Thirty-eight top-eight teams carried one. Look at who holds it. Inceneroar on 14 of those teams. Ferogeroth on nine. Pelipper on six. Those aren't the attackers. No. They're the Pokémon with a job to do. Fake Out and Intimidate, Trick Room, and Rain. Joseph Hugarte's Louisville-winning team put its Citrus Berry on Ferogeroth. The Trick Room setter. Citrus is on thirty-eight top-eight teams. The same is number seven, but number seven won more. Number seven is a weather setter. Drought, Drizzle, Sandstream, or Snow Warning. Only thirty-eight of the sixty-four top-eight teams ran one. Seven of the eight regional winners did. The only winning team without a weather setter was Eric Riosis at Frankfurt. Seven out of eight? Seven out of eight. Mega Charizard Y did most of it. It brought the sun on twenty-one top-eight teams. One R slash Pokémon Champions player summed it up as a weather setting ability and a hundred and fifty-nine base special attack. Then Rain. Pelipper on ten top-eight teams and Politoed on six. Sand from Mega Tyranitar on four. Snow from Mega Froslass on three. And Torkoal's Sun on two more. Eight top-eight teams ran two weather setters. Kirin Sing won Brisbane with Sun and Rain on the same team. Across all eight hundred and twenty-four lists, half had a weather setter. In the top eight, it was closer to six and ten. Number six is Focus Sash. On forty-five of the sixty-four top-eight teams and six of the eight winners. If the holder is at full HP, it survives a hit that would knock it out, with one HP left. It's single use. And if it never gets hit that hard, then it stays on. A top answer on R slash VGC spelled it out. The Sash is only used up when it activates. The most common Sash holders in the top eight were Sneasler on nine teams, Venusaur on eight, Whimsicott on seven, Hisui and Arcanine on five, and Excadrill on four. Venusaur shows the pattern. Chlorophyll doubles its speed in the Sun. In all eight top-eight Venusaur with a Sash ran Sleep Powder. The two with Life Orb didn't. Joseph Ugarte's Baltimore-winning team put its Sash on Excadrill next to Mega Tyranitar Sand. Number five is Speed Control. The two big ones. Tailwind or Trick Room. Forty-seven top-eight teams had at least one. Tailwind doubles your side's speed for four turns. Trick Room reverses the turn order for five, so the slowest Pokemon move first. Which one did the top-eight pick? Mostly Tailwind. Thirty-six top-eight teams ran it, against fifteen for Trick Room, and only four ran both. The Tailwind setters were spread out. Whimsicott on eight teams, then Megastaraptor, Pelipper and Megasalamence on seven each. Whimsicott's Prankster gives its status moves plus one priority, so its Tailwind goes up ahead of any attack without priority. Trick Room was the reverse of the Weather Story. It was on almost three in ten of all published lists, but fewer than one in four top-eight teams, and it won one regional, Louisville, with Fair Giraffe setting it. Number four is two Mega Stones on one team. You can only mega evolve once a battle. Right, and every one of the eight hundred twenty-four lists had at least one mega. Forty-eight of the sixty-four top-eight teams brought a second one anyway, a little less often than the field did. Kyle Livinghouse won Pueblo with Mega Charizard Y and Mega Froslass, and R slash VGC write-up of his team explained it. You'll only mega one of them, so in practice you pick your weather at team preview, sun or snow. Six of the eight regional winners brought two Mega Stones. The two that brought one, Recife and Louisville, both ran Mega Charizard Y. The most common megas in the top eight were Charizard Y on twenty-one teams, Mega Floette on seventeen, Mega Salamence on eleven, and Mega Garchob Z on ten. Number three is fake-out or intimidate. Fifty-one of the sixty-four top-eight teams had at least one of the two. Fake-out has plus three priority and makes the target flinch, but only on the user's first turnout. Intimidate lowers both opponents' attack by one stage when the user switches in. Each one was on forty-two top-eight teams. Fake-out came from Rillaboom on twenty-eight, Incineroar on twenty-four, and Sneezler on sixteen. That's more than one fake-out a team. A lot of the time, yes. Thirty-one top-eight teams ran two or more fake-out users. Intimidate came from Incineroar on twenty-four, Mega Salamence on eleven, and Mega Staraptor on seven. Salamence and Staraptor have Intimidate before they Mega Evolve. And no top-eight team ran more than one Intimidate user. Incineroar was the only Pokémon in the top eight that did both. Add Parting Shot and Citrus Berry, and it's part of three entries on this list. Number two is Protect, on at least four of your six Pokémon. Doesn't every team run Protect? Every one of the eight hundred and twenty-four lists had Protect or a move like it, such as Detect, Spiky Shield, or Baneful Bunker. One R slash VGC player called Protect, about as close as you can get to mandatory in doubles. The question is how many? Fifty-five of the sixty-four top-eight teams had it on four Pokémon or more, and most of those ran it on four or five. Javier Varela won Ganske with Protect on all six. Only one top-eight team ran it on just two. Daniel Walkers, sixth at Brisbane. Number one is a Spread Attack, a damaging move that hits both opponents at once, like Heat Wave, Rock Slide, Hyper Voice, or Make It Rain. Sixty-three of the sixty-four top-eight teams had one, including all eight regional winners. Most had two or three Pokémon carrying one. Mega Charizard Y led again on twenty-one teams, then Mega Floette on seventeen, Garchomp on fifteen, two of them as Mega Garchomp, and Goldengo on thirteen. The best line about it came from an R slash Pokémon Champions thread on Charizard Y. With a spread move boosted by the sun, you don't have to think too hard about which slot to attack. So who was the one team without one? Brady Smith, third at Baltimore, with Mega Gengar and Politoed both running Parasol. None of its attacks hit both opponents, and it still made the top four. Parasol makes every Pokémon on the field that hears it faint in three turns, unless it switches out. That's the full list. The spread attack, four protects, fake out or intimidate, two Megas, speed control, focus sash, weather, citrus berry, a pivot, and terrain. A few things showed up less in the top eight than across all lists, including trick room, redirection like Follow Me and Rage Powder, and Choice Scarf. Each of those three was on roughly three and ten published lists, but on fewer than one in four top eight teams. So those are bad now? Not bad. It's 64 teams, so treat it as a lean, not a rule. Trick room still won Louisville. Buenos Aires runs this weekend, and the Latin America International is the weekend after, both in this format. Check your team against the list before then.
