# Ranking Every New Mega Evolution Weakest to Strongest

**Channel:** Mystica | **Date:** unknown | **Views:** 4,129 | **Duration:** 22:26

---

Pokémon Legend ZA gave us over 40 new Megas, and with the new release of Pokémon Champions, we finally have access to 23 of these new Mega Evolutions for the first time in a game with traditional competitive Pokémon elements. This means we finally know the abilities for these new Pokémon for the first time ever. So today, let's see which of the new Megas ends up holding up in competitive Pokémon. We'll be ranking how every new Mega Evolution will do in terms of their performance in single battles for Pokémon Champions. So some Megas that are going to be standouts in a double setting may rank lower due to this. With that out of the way, let's start off with our worst of the new Megas. Starting off our list is a rather disappointing Mega Evolution, Mega Emboar. While Mega Emboar isn't the literal worst when it comes to stats and typing, there is a massive opportunity cost when using the Mega Evolution of Emboar instead of Base Emboar, and that is the loss of Rekkles. When Mega Evolving Emboar, your new ability ends up being Mold Breaker, which really lacks a legitimate use on a Pokémon like Emboar. You're losing a significant portion of your damage, especially from Flare Blitz, by using the Mega Stone on Emboar over not Mega Evolving in the first place. Even Base Emboar, without a damaging item, does more damage than Mega Emboar when using the move Flare Blitz, which is its primary damaging option. While Champions lacks items like the Choice Spand and Life Orb, when these do eventually return to the games, it'll only become a more substantial difference, with the Choice Spand making Mega Emboar truly useless when it comes to using moves like Close Combat. While Mega Emboar has some cool aspects about it, this is easily the biggest downgrade you get from using a Mega Stone on a Pokémon, and it makes options like Garchomp look silly, forever worrying about their bad Mega Evolutions. While Mega Emboar was a downgrade, Mega Victory Bell isn't exactly much better. Despite being a more lateral move for Victory Bell, Victory Bell still ends up being a fairly unimpressive Mega. You lose Chlorophyll for Innards Out, which has some interesting ratifications. Victory Bell was really only even potentially useful if it got Chlorophyll for an ability as a counterpart to Base Venusaur, that could have at least been given some better offenses in bulk. However, with Innards Out, you're essentially just using this to trade KOs by sacking your own Mega. While this does allow for some interesting KOs against options like Mega Charizard Y that are really terrifying in the current format, you need to ensure you're throwing your Mega Victory Bell out from full HP while already Mega Evolved, or the Innards Out gimmick ends up falling flat for you. Similarly, Mega Skull Villain ranks slightly higher than Mega Victory Bell for basically the same issues. This is another Grass-type Mega from ZA that should have had Chlorophyll to make it even somewhat viable, but at least Mega Skull Villain has a much more terrifying dual typing of Fire and Grass. Also the ability Spicy Spray, being able to burn an enemy anytime you're hit, regardless if it's by a physical or special move, is at least a more useful ability that can be activated a lot more within your control instead of banking on your enemy KOing a Mega Victory Bell from full HP. Mega Skull Villain is still really bad, but it's a slight step above the previous two entries. Mega Hawlucha is another Mega that, similar to Emboar, proves to be a substantial downgrade to its predecessor. Mega Hawlucha at least has some genuine niche with its ability though, as Noguard allows both High Jump Kick and Dual Wing Beat to be utilized without worrying about their accuracy. But this isn't exactly making up for the fact Mega Hawlucha was by far best utilized as an Unburdened option. There wasn't ever really a world this could have been a better option than Base Hawlucha though, and at least it gives Hawlucha a more defined niche given Sneezler's existence, giving us a far greater upgrade on the Unburdened Fighting type role anyway. So this isn't as big of an impact. So at least Base Hawlucha's damage isn't an upgrade on Mega Hawlucha, which gives you a potential reason to actually want to use your Mega Evolution in the first place. Pokemon Champions has a lot of fast Psychic type Megas, with our worst of the batch being Mega Meowstic. This is a pretty cut and dry option, since they essentially made Mega Alakazam, but bad. At the very least, Mega Meowstic is the first Mega today that is a genuine upgrade to the base Pokemon, and it's honestly a substantial one at that. Mega Meowstic gives us a genuine upgrade in offenses to our base Meowstic, and it has enough tools between its pre-mega prankster or competitive that you can utilize in order to make it a far more dangerous tool once you've used your Mega. Also, Trace as an ability has proven to be dangerous depending on the scenario, but it's not exactly the most consistent ability on a Mega, as you need to position it right in order for it to be impactful at all. If Mega Meowstic wasn't competing with another faster and stronger Psychic type that also had Trace, or multiple other fast Psychic type Megas with better typings, maybe it could've actually had a chance to shine. But it's just too outclassed by other options, unfortunately. Mega Drampa is honestly a pretty big oddity in Champions, and I'm unsure exactly how good it'll be. Similar to Meowstic, this is a Pokemon that got a pretty substantial increase to its stats by using its Mega Stone, and Mega Drampa will definitely see some increased usage due to this. However, Normal and Dragon isn't exactly the best typing, as Normal doesn't really offer anything to the Dragon type defensively or offensively speaking. That said though, as a Normal type, Mega Drampa has some pretty amazing coverage with options like Fire Blast, Earth Power, Thunderbolt, and Energy Ball to name a few. You can easily make Mega Drampa into a monstrous wall breaker, but the typing and speed I think hold it back outside of some niche situations on Trick Room. This was arguably the worst Pokemon before a Mega evolution on our list, but Mega Chimecho actually proved to be a satisfying buff to the base form. Mega Chimecho fixes a few key issues that base Chimecho had, with the first being it now finally has a viable typing due to the addition of the Steel typing. Mega Chimecho also has stats that finally are worthy of respect due to its significant increases to Bulk and Special Attack. They actually put a lot of care making sure Mega Chimecho could stand on its own two feet, or I guess float. As the ability Levitate pairs really well defensively with this new typing as well to reduce its ground weakness. There are unfortunately too many Dark types that are at the top of the format though, which will be a huge hindrance for Mega Chimecho, but it did at least finally become somewhat usable. Unlike ZA's base starters, the Kalos starters all ended up getting treated really well by their Megas. Mega Chestnut however, ends up still being the worst of the three. Mega Chestnut does basically exactly what base Chestnut does, but outright better in every way. Whether you want to use it as an Iron Defense body press option or a spike lead, Mega Chestnut will just be able to perform the role significantly better due to its significant increases to its defensive stats. Mega Chestnut also keeps the ability Bulletproof, which while not the most lethal, can perform well against Poison types of Sludge Bomb that would otherwise hit it for super effective damage. Mega Chestnut is probably the first Mega on our list today that I could genuinely see having a place in the Champion's metagame, regardless of the viable mods. Mega Floette is a Pokemon that almost ended up being outright overpowered in so many regards, but thankfully Game Freak decided to actually balance an otherwise really powerful Mega. Mega Floette was given a statline that in tandem with Fairy Aura allows it to out damage even Xerneas, while still giving it setup and even a base 140 power Fairy move Light of Ruin. This deals horrifying amounts of damage to opponents. Mega Floette is, however, held back by the fact it has no real way to damage opposing steels, which even with our limited decks, there are still countless steels that would pose a threat in singles. Options like Corviknight, Mega Skarmory, Mega Excadrill, Archiladon, Mega Lucario, and Adislash are a few of the many options that would really easily handle Mega Floette. That said, Mega Floette can at least try to outmaneuver these if it's given proper time to set up. So I would still put a lot of respect on this Mega if you see it in battles. We actually got multiple Mega evolutions that perform really well under Trick Room, and the first is Golurk. Mega Golurk got a few really key tools and champions to become a potent wall breaker, with the first being a strong buff to its attack in bulk. Also, Mega Golurk was given Headlong Rush as the champions, allowing it to deal more significant damage with a 120 base power Ground type move. This is a pretty big deal for Mega Golurk to secure KOs against some bulkier Pokemon. Making sure that you can properly maximize your Trick Room terms to the best of your ability. While Mega Golurk is by no means a top Mega, it definitely proves to be a pretty terrifying option for any team. Mega Crabominable proves to be in a fairly similar situation to Mega Golurk. Mega Crabominable also got some pretty strong buffs to its offenses in bulk. But where this Mega shines is the reduction to its speed, allowing it to perform as an even slower wall breaker for Trick Room teams. This in tandem with its defensive increases and the monstrous offensive typing of Ice in fighting should be a huge buff to a Pokemon that previously saw very little play. Also, the addition of priority options like First Impression and Mach Punch should help in case you can't guarantee Trick Room stays on your side of the field to secure a last-ditch KO or two. While this Pokemon has seen a lot of its best play as a focus sash hazard setter, Mega Glomora brings a lot of very strong offensive buffs to Glomora. With the addition of adaptability as well as buffs to your special attack and speed, Mega Glomora sits at a pretty respectable 101 base speed stat, which is great to outpace the very common base 100 speed Pokemon. Adaptability on a Pokemon with Rock and Poison typing allows for Pokemon that aren't steel types to be hit for pretty substantial damage. And you can always partner these stabs with options like Earth Power to deal some pretty good coverage damage. I could see Mega Glomora also being an option to pivot around pre-mega to get toxic spikes up with its ability Toxic Debris before then actually using its mega to deal damage once you've ensured that your opponent is stuck taking passive damage from the poison. Mega Chandelure is easily one of the best wall breakers that Legend ZA gave us for Megas. A mega with base 175 special attack is pretty obnoxious. And Mega Chandelure is also benefiting significantly from its potent fire and ghost typing, which provides it a lot of strong offensive and defensive benefits. While I think Mega Chandelure will struggle against hazard stacking enemies using rocks or spikes to damage you easily, you'll still be able to deal significant damage even against resistances. Also, if you throw this on a team with options like Sun or Sticky Webs, you'll be able to provide Mega Chandelure even more support to truly clean through everything your opponent is utilizing. Mega Excadrill was a really hard mega evolution to get wrong. But I wouldn't necessarily say this was a mega that was handled as well as it should have. Mega Excadrill got a pretty strong buff to its attack and speed. And if they gave it the ability Sand Rush, this honestly would have been our number one choice. However, Piercing Drill allows Mega Excadrill the ability to deal one fourth damage through Protect, which isn't exactly noteworthy in singles. Even if we were ranking doubles, Mega Excadrill really needed Sand Rush. A 103 speed tier would have been so clutch for it to actually use this ability. Since even when the Paradox Pokemon get introduced in the future, this would have been perfect for creeping options like Iron Bundle after a booster speed. On the bright side, Mega Excadrill can at least still be a strong offensive Pokemon, as well as a decent Rapid Spinner. But you'd likely be better using a Focus Sash or Soft Sand on Base Excadrill and take advantage of Mold Breaker or Sand Rush instead. This is arguably going to be the Mega that will cause the most controversy. But Mega Dragonite was another Mega that didn't really do much to improve Base Dragonite's good qualities. While Mega Dragonite does have a new niche as a hurricane option for rain that also carries moves like Thunder and Draco Meteor for strong damage, you objectively lost out on a lot of what made Base Dragonite really good. Whether you're using Inner Focus with physical moves to avoid Intimidate or Multiscale as a pre-Mega to take advantage of Boots, you just can't really get these benefits from Mega Dragonite. Even Dragon Dance sets just feel weaker because the Mega actually should be used as a special attacker instead. Mega Dragonite at least feels a little less bad due to heavy duty boots not currently being in champions. But they will inevitably be re-added to the game, which will only make Mega Dragonite feel even worse, as this was a huge part of Base Dragonite's identity to preserve Multiscale against team stacking hazards. By no means is Mega Dragonite bad, but if they were going to make it a special attacker, it really needed a different ability. Mega Feraligatr is a pretty interesting Mega. This is the first of our Megas that I would argue ends up being something I would say you need to actually watch out for in terms of prep, as its offenses paired with the ability Dragonize are nothing to scoff at. That said, Mega Feraligatr is a bit slow, with Base 78's speed not exactly cutting it like it used to back in older generations. Even after a Dragon Dance, you're still barely able to outpace options like Dragapult. Mega Feraligatr would benefit a lot from Sticky Webs, but they could also easily be what leads it to being KO'd in return. Despite this though, there are very few Pokemon that will appreciate taking a plus one double edge with a Dragonize boost for Mega Feraligatr, as even most Steels will be at least two hit KO'd by this. Also, the Water Dragon typing defensively is so strong that I still see a strong place for this in the metagame as a great physical wall breaker. Mega Skarmory proves to be a pretty interesting Mega in Champions 2. While its new ability, Stalwart, is outright useless in singles, it got a pretty strong overhaul and was a Mega that got its identity redefined properly. Pre-Mega, you can use Mega Skarmory as a strong option to set up moves like Swords Dance and utilize the ability Weak Armor to gain a strong speed boost when being hit by a physical attack. This way, when you use your Mega evolution, you pretty quickly have a Pokemon with 140 attack and 110 speed that can also have plus two boosts to both its speed and attack. Between your Steel and Flying stabs, as well as coverage like Drill Run, Mega Skarmory can actually prove to be a pretty strong offensive Mega that has a lot of versatility in how you utilize it best on any given team. Mega Meganium being ranked this high might surprise a lot of you guys, but of the ZA starters, this definitely is the Mega I'd say that got the best treatment. Mega Meganium, now becoming a Grass Fairy type Pokemon, is a pretty incredible buff for it offensively. Mega Meganium also got some much needed buffs to its defenses and special attack to finally make it a Mega that should warrant your respect. But what makes Mega Meganium a true threat is the ability Mega Soul that allows you to always take advantage of the benefits of Sun being activated regardless of what weather is on the field. Mega Meganium now gets access to one turn Solar Beams, as well as Weather Ball always being a Fire type move, which allows for you to use these in tandem with Dazzling Gleam and Earth Power for perfect coverage into everything. You can also sub out one of these moves for an option like Synthesis, which restores two thirds of Meganium's health once you Mega Evolve it. And from there, it basically becomes unbeatable. They for sure wanted to give Meganium its chance in the spotlight. And I could see this genuinely being a strong option. We're finally at the top five best new Megas and champions with our first contender being Mega Clefable. Mega Clefable was another Mega that similar to options like Excadrill and Dragonite missed out on a few key things to make it truly broken. But this was by far the Pokemon with the best base form that still proves to be a strong option even after Mega Evolving. Starting off with the really good factors, Mega Clefable gave Clefable a much needed stat overhaul to all of its stats, allowing it to finally have really strong defensive and offensive stats. Base Clefable can also take advantage of its pure fairy typing with either Magic Guard or Unaware, both being really strong options in their own way, while then getting access to Magic Bounce after Mega Evolving to redirect any hazards or status that may punish your team. The addition of your flying type as well also makes Mega Clefable into an even better defensive option for teams, as fairy and flying have immunities to two of the best types in the game, Dragon and Ground. Mega Clefable's only real drawback is the lack of a good flying move after Mega Evolving, since it otherwise was handled really well. Mega Delphox was a fast psychic type Mega done right, as they allowed it to actually get a proper identity that isn't just a Mega Alakazam clone. While it is still a fast psychic type special attacker, similar to Miastik and Alakazam, the addition of the fire type, as well as a unique ability levitate, helps Mega Delphox quite a bit with at least giving it a unique niche in the format. Mega Delphox is your fast nasty plot special attacker that you'd wanna use on a Sun team, which at minimum is a way to define it a bit more from just being outright worse than Mega Alakazam when it does eventually come the champions. Also, the immunity of the spikes and webs will be huge for Mega Delphox to maintain a strong viability in single battles. Mega Greninja was obviously going to be a top five Mega, considering they didn't really change anything that made base Greninja good. It's quite literally protein Greninja with faster speed and offenses. It's quite unfortunate that they did cut nasty plot, but you can still use Mega Greninja as a fast option to clean their opponents with ease. Unlike previous entries that would either prefer a life orb or choice item to increase damage, Mega Greninja's speed allows it to outrun a lot of the base 130 Megas while still dealing increased damage compared to its base form. So this actually is a Mega that is worth just using the stone as opposed to options like Dragon Knight, Emboar and Hawlucha that just benefit from not ever Mega Evolving in their matches in the first place. One of the best Megas in this game is easily Mega Froslass. Mega Froslass got quite a bit of buffs that allow it to perform as a genuinely top tier option for any team and champions. With the additional special attack, speed and access to Snow Warning, Mega Froslass proves to be a Mega that just demands your respect. This usually would require at least having some alternate weather on your team to escape it from just spamming Blizzard and nasty plot into your team as a means of destroying anything in your way. While it's by no means the best offensively speaking, the additional bulk with both your increased stats and the snow boosting your physical defense allows Mega Froslass to also be a bit annoying to actually KO. Meaning you'll have ample time to set up and just go for the win in case your opponent's not already in range yet. To no one's surprise, our number one new Mega from champions is easily Mega Starmie. While Mega Starmie lost out on some key tools like Triple Axle that helped it quite a bit in viability, we at least still keep Ice Spinner as an important form of coverage. That said, a Mega with 100 attack, 120 speed and huge power is always just going to be good. Mega Starmie has access to options like Bulk Up to boost it's already obnoxious attack while also using offensive stab like Flip Turn, Zen Headbutt, Aqua Jet and Liquidation to wall break enemies with ease. While Mega Starmie doesn't exactly have the best coverage, when you're dealing this much damage, it doesn't really matter too much. And with that, we've officially ranked every new Mega in champions.
