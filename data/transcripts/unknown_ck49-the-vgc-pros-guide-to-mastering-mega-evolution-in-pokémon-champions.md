# The VGC Pro's Guide to Mastering Mega Evolution in Pokémon Champions

**Channel:** ck49 | **Date:** unknown | **Views:** 2,356 | **Duration:** 10:03

---

Pokémon Champions is out, and mega evolutions are finally back. It has been too long since we've been able to use them in competitive play, and I am really really really excited for their return. And while I can't tell you just yet which mega evolution to use or how to use any given Pokémon to its fullest potential because, you know, the game literally just released, what I can tell you is some helpful tips and tricks that I've learned through over a decade of playing competitive Pokémon about how to best utilize and build around your mega evolution of choice. But first, let me give you the basics. A mega evolution is a powered-up version of a Pokémon. It requires a mega stone to mega evolve and also requires you to actually press the mega evolution button in game on the turn that you are attacking with it. Usually, these Pokémon get 100 points added to their base stat total, and many of them also get new and powerful abilities. You can also mega evolve one Pokémon per battle, but you can have multiple Pokémon holding mega stones on your team if you so choose. Which brings me to the first thing that I want to talk about, which is how many megas should you have on your team? And the answer to that is almost always just one, but we can talk about the exceptions. The reason that you usually only want one Pokémon holding a mega stone on your team is because there is a huge opportunity cost to holding the mega stone. A mega Pokémon, despite the increase in power, does not get to hold an item, and there are a whole lot of really good items in VGC. So, if you have to bring a Pokémon holding a mega stone to a battle and not mega evolve it because maybe you brought a second mega Pokémon that you want to mega evolve, then you are missing out on the benefit of a generally really powerful item on that Pokémon. But in VGC, you only bring four of your six Pokémon to a match, so generally speaking in team building, you want to make sure your team has a cohesive game plan around any combination of four Pokémon that you might want to bring together. So, if you do want to put two megas on a team, the most important thing that you can do is have four other Pokémon on your team, of which you can bring three of them with either mega evolution, thereby avoiding a situation where you bring both mega Pokémon in a one game. One great example of this from me personally is I used in 2018 a mega Gengar mega Latios team, which is of course mega Latios is not in Pokémon Champions, but in 2018, as well as probably right now, mega Gengar teams had a big weakness to sun teams built around mega Charizard Y, but mega Latios was really really good against that specific team. And so, if you know there are specific matchups where your primary mega is not too good, the that's the perfect opportunity to find a second mega evolution that patches those specific megas that you otherwise have a hard time with. I also want to point out that James Baek top cut the World Championships back in 2019 with no megas on his team, but that was a very high power level format with Pokémon like Groudon and Kyogre legal, so he had other powerful options to choose from. Champions has very few other Pokémon that can match the power level of the strongest mega evolutions, so you probably should have at least one mega on your team at least to start out. Also, can you imagine waiting 7 years to play with mega evolutions and then deciding, "Eh, I'm going to pass." Don't do that. One of my favorite things about team building in a format with mega evolutions is that you get to team build around the unique strengths of your mega evolution. And so, obviously, if your team is built around, say, Charizard Y or Froslass, which set up weather, then you should be able to take advantage of the sun or snow that those guys set up. I don't think I need to explain those, but for the most part, your mega is your hardest hitter, and a lot of them don't do a lot to support your team other than hit very hard, Pokémon like mega Metagross, for example. And so, you want to build around the type of damage that your mega does and make sure its partners have the right offensive coverage to stop your opponent from walling your mega evolution. For example, mega Gardevoir's ability is Pixilate, which allows it to fire off really powerful fairy type hyper voices, which hits both of your opponent's Pokémon. Your opponent is naturally going to want to switch in their fire or steel type in front of that, or maybe their poison type, but you can see why that would be a bad idea in front of the psychic type Gardevoir. But if you pair Gardevoir with a strong ground type Pokémon, say, then not only can you hit both of your opponent's Pokémon hard with a hyper voice, but also cover for any potential switches with the ground type move. This is called a pin, and it's a topic I made a video about years ago that is still relevant, but to quickly summarize, a pin is a strong spread move that is combined with a single target move that covers its weaknesses. And given that basically every single mega in the game is either clicking a strong spread move or a single target move that's also very powerful, figuring out what partners can create pins next to it is a really important part of team building with megas. If you want a good template for how to team build with a mega, I would suggest starting with one mega evolution, two partner Pokémon that can create strong pins with it, ideally different typings, and then two support Pokémon that allow you to get in position to abuse those pins, say maybe a Tailwind user to allow your Pokémon to move first, or a fake out user to give you a free turn to set up. And then finally, you do have one flex slot, which you can use to patch whatever matchups you find yourself struggling with. That makes it easy to create game plans where you always have your mega, at least one Pokémon to create a pin with it, at least one support Pokémon, and then whatever you happen to need in that last slot. And if you decide you want more team building advice, I have a video called the best VGC team building advice that you will ever get, and it's like the most viewed video on my channel, so I think it's pretty good, and yeah, you can decide for yourself. I've also been asked a couple questions about how to best utilize your mega when you're actually playing, starting with do you ever not bring your mega to a match? Which is a really fair question to ask, and the answer is yes, but ideally as sparingly as possible. In formats where there are higher power level Pokémon, it's easier to justify doing because you need to make sure that you have enough damage to win the game if you drop your biggest damage dealer. For example, if you have a rain team with both, say, a mega Dragonite clicking Hurricane, as well as an Archaludon, then you can justify doing it because once it gets going, the Archaludon can easily win you the game. But if you are running a sun team, you probably should always bring your Charizard to set up sun. This comes back to team building and finding combinations of four Pokémon that can work together well. You don't need every combination of four Pokémon on your team to work together, but you probably need like three to five solid modes on a good team, and if one of those involves situationally benching your mega, that's okay. But remember that this is probably the strongest Pokémon on your team, so I personally would rather build my teams in a way where I can always bring my mega evolution and can cover for its weaknesses with my other Pokémon, or maybe add a second mega if I'm finding that hard to do in some specific cases. I've also been asked questions like, "Should I always mega right away, or should I always lead my mega Pokémon?" And again, both of those are very team and context dependent. Well, let's talk about that context because usually I will mega my Pokémon right away. For a Pokémon like Gengar, there's basically no downside to doing that. On the other hand, Gyarados will often choose to not mega right away because it has Intimidate, which it might want to use more than once in a battle. Similarly, it loses its flying typing for a dark typing, which in some matches can be a negative thing. So, sometimes you want to prioritize knocking out your opponent's fighting or ground type Pokémon and then mega evolve your Gyarados. But it's worth remembering that you do get a whole lot of stats both in bulk and damage output when you choose to mega evolve, so if you need to switch in your mega before it's mega evolved, either because you didn't lead it or because you switched it out before you mega'd, then you are going to take a lot more damage when you switch it back in, again, barring something weather setters can have the most complicated relationship with choosing when to mega, and I'm looking at Tyranitar specifically because there are times where you want to delay setting your weather up. Sometimes you want to take advantage of those five turns of your weather a little later in the game. And Tyranitar is, again, a weird example because it not only sets up sand when it switches in, but then also when it mega evolves, sand stream sets up sand once again if sand is not already active. So, it can be worth delaying your mega until either your opponent turns off your sand or until your sand expires after five turns, and that way you can reset it without having to switch your Tyranitar out and back in. This is a strategy that I am especially partial to because my first regional top cut ever was back in 2018 using a sand team with mega Tyranitar, and I remember often holding mega until I really needed to use it.
