# Pokemon Champions Stats, EVs, and Natures Simplified

**Channel:** Reno | **Date:** unknown | **Views:** 324 | **Duration:** 2:59

---

Hello hello and welcome, Pokemon Champions just dropped, a free to play Pokemon battler that is available on your switches and mobile devices. Lots of people may be starting off their interest in Pokemon here. If you're someone like that, one of the core mechanics to learn that really opens up the complexity and strategy of this seemingly simple game is the entire stat system. Pokemon stats used to be split 5 ways, HP, Attack, Defense, Special, and Speed. The Special stat used to determine a Pokemon's Attack and Defense from getting hit by a special move from Generations 1 to 3. Gen 4 and onwards, we have the 6 stats like most people have recognized from nowadays, with Special Attack and Special Defense being the newcomers making our 5 stats now 6. Every Pokemon species has something called the fake stats, this is what that Pokemon is naturally good at. Some are about to hit hard, some are about to take hits, and some are just trying their best. If you're not playing to that Pokemon's strength, you're basically nerfing yourself without realizing it. But here's where things get weird, even if two of the exact same Pokemon go head to head, they can still have completely different stats. And that's usually because of EVs and IVs, but with Pokemon Champions there will not be any IVs or individual values for Pokemon Champions to maintain competitive integrity and eliminate the grind of breeding slash catching. Think of this as a nice quality of life update, and hence we will only go over EVs or effort values. This is where the training comes in. In the mainline games, every Pokemon you battle gives you hidden stat points based on what you find. Every 4 EVs gets you 1 point into a designated stat. If you fight fast Pokemon, you get faster, if you fight bulk Pokemon, you get bulkier. You could also use items like HP Ups or Attack Ups to raise them too. Two of the same Pokemon could have different speed EVs that would typically make what would be a speed tie into a decisive moment where your EV training could make your Pokemon just 1 point faster, making you go first and win the battle. In another quality of life update, you will have a slider to adjust your EVs however you want at the cost of Victory Points or VP, the currency of the game. And just to make things even more finely tuned, every Pokemon also has a nature. Matures boost one stat at the cost of lowering another, so you can have a strong attacker that randomly gets a nature that makes it worse at attacking. While your Pokemon's personality can mess up your entire build, this is another change that you can make for more VP, points you earn for battles as you play the game. When you stack this all together, base stats, EVs, and nature, you start to realize something, that there is a lot that goes into crafting your Pokemon from which to use, the nature they have, and the EVs they utilize, and that's before considering 4 moves and 5 whole other Pokemon to repeat this for a team. The important thing is this, while there is a lot to take in to start, Pokemon Champions makes this process boatloads easier than it used to be to formulate a team, even including transferring your favorite buddies from older games to visit from Pokemon Home or to recruit them from the game itself. Come on in and give it a shot, as it is a perfect time to get into battling Pokemon in the Gargantuan 2-6.
