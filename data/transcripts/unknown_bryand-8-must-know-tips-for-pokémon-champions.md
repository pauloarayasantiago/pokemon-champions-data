Pokémon Champions finally releases on mobile later this week and here's eight must know tips to help you on your journey. Number one, complete the tutorials. I know this might sound like a no-brainer, but let's be honest, we all hate tutorials. However, the game will give you some really nice rewards for completing these tutorials and they're relatively fast. My personal favorite tutorial is completing the mega evolution one because this one gets you eight free mega stones. Now Pokémon Champions, you can play totally for free and you don't need to have any other Pokémon games to play this. However, I do highly recommend bringing Pokémon into this game by using Pokémon Home. If you've [music] ever played Pokémon Go, you can easily import Pokémon from Pokémon Go into Pokémon Home and then into Pokémon Champions. A Pokémon's previous stats, including its CP or level, do not matter in Pokémon Champions at all. Speaking of the Pokémon themselves, using the recruit function to gain new Pokémon in your roster is a great way to test out new Pokémon and strategies. You get one free pull every day where you're shown 10 different Pokémon total [music] and you're able to choose one to recruit by using your VP to get them permanently or by using a recruit ticket if you have any available so you don't spend your VP. Pokémon recruited from the ranch even have a small chance to be shiny. So, if you do a recruit and you don't like any of the options that you see, you don't have to spend any VP to permanently recruit anything if you don't want them. Instead, you can temporarily recruit a Pokémon by picking trial recruit and this will cost you no VP at all and you'll still be able to use the Pokémon for 7 days. The only other downside to this is that you will not be able to edit that Pokémon stats at all. You can also influence the batch of Pokémon recruited at the ranch by using an affinity ticket. You can get each types affinity tickets by using that specific types moves 50 times in battles and 250 times in battles. Here is a water type affinity ticket that I pulled and you can see I got four different water types in this batch. Now earlier I mentioned the tutorials, but there's also beginner missions that you can take on as well. These will give you a ton of resources early on and they're super simple to complete. Definitely do these missions in addition to your dailies and weekly missions as well. Next, let's talk about training your Pokémon. Training your Pokémon in this game costs VP or victory points. This is a currency that you get by completing missions and by battling. There's also training tickets, a somewhat rare item that you can get in this game by leveling up through the battle pass or you can get a whole bunch by just buying the starter pack for real life money. Make sure you spend your VP wisely. The larger the adjustment to a Pokémon that you make, the more VP it will cost you. Consider using a training ticket on these costly changes to a Pokémon instead of using your VP. This way you can use your VP on other important things like your character's outfit [music] and the battle music, the actually important stuff. And lastly, if you have zero clue of how to train your Pokémon, this game actually allows you to see the battle data of what's currently being used on each Pokémon. For example, if you want to know what to run on your Blastoise, you can click on one of its moves and then click the button for battle data and then you can see what moves are being used in both single battles and double battles. It's very helpful and you can see this data for every single trainable attribute of your Pokémon. Guys, as always, thank you so much for watching the video.
